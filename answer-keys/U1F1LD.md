## Grading Vocabulary

- **Follow-test:** Decide whether the stated reason, together with any defensible hidden assumption, supports the conclusion.
- **Stable-term test:** Decide whether a key word keeps the same meaning throughout an argument.
- **Stable rewrite:** Keep one clear meaning of the shifting term throughout a rewrite. A stable rewrite may still be invalid; valid limited repair is a separate job unless the prompt requires it.
- **Scope test:** Decide whether All, None, Some, or Most is no broader than the evidence supports.
- **Route consequence:** State what a solved logic problem permits the expedition to record about a door, including what remains unproved.
- **Relevant evidence:** Evidence that bears on which passage is most likely to rise toward natural daylight.
- **Distractor:** A fact or object that attracts attention but does not help answer the route question.
- **Repair:** Revise a claim by stabilizing a term, narrowing its scope, or correcting its inference without abandoning its subject.
- **Carry-forward reasoning:** Score an earlier error where it occurs. In later synthesis, award credit for reasoning consistently from the student's recorded result while using other evidence accurately; do not deduct twice for one misconception. Because the evaluator may not see earlier boxes, a noncanonical later choice earns carry-forward synthesis credit only when the current answer explicitly states the earlier premise or classification it relies on.

## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Laurel argument diagnosis (3 points)

**Evidence cutoff:** Laurel Chorus inscription only (applause for a hundred years → Laurel is the wisest road to daylight). Do not use later creature, compass, or map evidence.

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Conclusion: Laurel is the wisest road to daylight (or the wise road toward daylight). Reason: travelers have applauded Laurel for a hundred years. Hidden assumption: long/widespread applause or popularity proves a road is wise or leads to daylight. Follows: No — applause establishes approval/popularity/tradition, not wisdom, safety, or daylight direction.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Assumption wording may be “approval means wise,” “popular routes are correct,” or “a century of applause proves the best road.” Conclusion may be phrased as “Laurel is wise/best/right for daylight.” Ordinary “does not follow / no” is enough when explained.
- **Required logical jobs:** (1) conclusion, (2) stated reason, (3) hidden assumption, (4) follow-test with brief explanation that applause ≠ wisdom/daylight.

**Rubric:**
- 3 pts: All four jobs present and correct
- 2 pts: Correct “does not follow” judgment with one part vague or missing, or all parts present with thin explanation
- 1 pt: Recognizes that applause is not proof of wisdom/daylight but does not parse conclusion/reason/assumption
- 0 pts: Accepts the argument, invents directional proof from applause, or missing

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the words “hidden assumption” if the bridging claim is clearly stated.
- Do not require later moth/compass analysis here.

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – Echo equivocation and rewrite (3 points)

**Evidence cutoff:** Echo Door guide inscription only. Do not require naturalist or ledger evidence.

**Prompt jobs (worksheet):** Identify the word that changes meaning; explain both meanings; rewrite the argument with one stable meaning; decide what follows.

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical shifting term:** *sound*.
- **Canonical meanings:** (1) structurally solid / uncracked / free of cracks (mason inspection of the first chamber); (2) wise, reasonable, trustworthy, or good as a full-route decision toward daylight.
- **Canonical model rewrite (valid limited):** “Masons found no cracks in Echo’s first chamber; therefore Echo’s first chamber has no observed cracks.” Limited conclusion follows; “Echo is the road to daylight” does not.
- **Acceptable alternatives for meanings:** “physically solid / no cracks” vs “good idea / reliable choice / trustworthy route”; “structurally OK” vs “the right decision.”
- **Stable rewrite (required by prompt):** Any rewrite that keeps *sound* (or substitutes one clear sense) in one meaning throughout. Stability does **not** require narrowing to a valid limited conclusion.
- **Stable but still-invalid rewrite with accurate retest (full credit on jobs 1–4):** e.g. keep *sound* = structurally solid throughout: “Echo’s first chamber is structurally sound (no cracks). Every structurally sound choice deserves trust. Therefore Echo is the structurally sound road to daylight.” Student then correctly says the daylight/trust conclusion still does not follow (trust premise unsupported; first-chamber condition ≠ whole-route daylight road). This earns full credit even though the rewrite is not the key’s valid limited model.
- **Valid limited repair (also full credit):** Narrow to the inspected chamber / no-observed-cracks claim, as in the canonical model, and retest accurately.
- **Continuing shift (not full credit on rewrite):** Using structural “sound” in one premise and “wise/good decision” in another, or sliding into “sound road to daylight” without fixing one sense.
- **Required logical jobs:** (1) identify *sound* (or clearly the shifting word), (2) both meanings, (3) one stable-meaning rewrite, (4) accurate retest of what follows after stabilization.

**Rubric:**
- 3 pts: Both senses, a stable rewrite (valid-limited **or** stable-but-invalid), and accurate retest
- 2 pts: Correct senses and “original daylight conclusion fails,” but rewrite still shifts, is missing, or retest is thin
- 1 pt: Identifies *sound* / ambiguity without clear rewrite and retest
- 0 pts: Misses the shift, treats the original as following, or missing

**Notes for grading:**
- Do **not** require a “stable limited rewrite,” valid form, or narrowed evidential repair unless the student chooses that path. The worksheet asks for stable meaning + what follows, not Step-5 argument repair.
- Do not require the words “equivocation” or “ambiguous middle.”
- Inventing “every sound chamber deserves trust” as an established fact is a defect if treated as proved; if used inside a stable rewrite that the student then correctly rejects, the retest can still earn credit.
- Do not use Part 4 naturalist facts to grade this box.
