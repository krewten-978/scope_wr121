## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Right to speak vs. right about policy (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**  
- **Canonical word:** *right*.
- **Canonical first meaning:** A claim, entitlement, permission, or liberty to speak at the meeting.
- **Canonical second meaning:** Correct, accurate, or true about the policy.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “legal/moral right” versus “factually correct,” “allowed to speak” versus “having the correct position,” or similarly clear wording.
- **Required meaning:** The response must identify the repeated word and distinguish permission/entitlement from correctness/truth.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Identifies *right* and clearly states both distinct meanings in context
- 0 pts: Omits the word or either meaning, treats both uses as the same meaning, or gives a materially different distinction

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the technical words “entitlement,” “permission,” or “correct”; ordinary language is sufficient if the distinction is unambiguous.
- The first use is not the “correct answer” sense, and the second use is not a speaking privilege.

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – Free country vs. free admission (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**  
- **Canonical word:** *free*.
- **Canonical first meaning:** Politically free, self-governing, or possessing civil liberty rather than being under oppressive control.
- **Canonical second meaning:** Available without charge or an admission fee.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “liberty/freedom from tyranny” versus “costs no money,” “independent country” versus “free of charge,” or similarly clear contextual distinctions.
- **Required meaning:** The response must identify the repeated word and distinguish political liberty from price.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Identifies *free* and clearly states both distinct meanings in context
- 0 pts: Omits the word or either meaning, treats both uses as identical, or confuses the political and cost senses

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the phrase “political liberty” or “self-governing.” “People have rights/freedom” is sufficient for the first sense when contrasted clearly with “no admission fee.”
- “Open to the public” alone is too vague for the second sense unless the response makes clear that admission costs nothing.

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – Feathers are light (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**  
- **Canonical shifting term:** *light*.
- **Canonical meanings:** (1) low in weight/not heavy, as applied to feathers; (2) bright, illuminated, or not dark, as used in the first premise.
- **Canonical failure explanation:** The conclusion transfers the brightness sense to feathers even though the premise about feathers uses only the low-weight sense; low weight does not imply brightness or inability to be dark.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “lightweight” versus “giving/containing light,” “not heavy” versus “opposite of dark,” or other wording that preserves the weight/illumination distinction.
- **Required meaning:** The response must identify the term, both contextual meanings, and the unlicensed shift that breaks the inference.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Correctly identifies *light*, states both meanings, and explains that the conclusion depends on switching from weight to brightness
- 1 pt: Correctly identifies *light* and both meanings but does not explain the failed inference; or identifies the term and gives a clear failure explanation while leaving one meaning only implied
- 0 pts: Gives one or fewer required components, treats the two meanings as equivalent, or offers an unrelated reason the conclusion fails

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the formal term “equivocation” because the question asks students to identify and explain the shift rather than name the fallacy.
- “Feathers can be dark even though they weigh little” is a sufficient concise explanation when both meanings are otherwise clear.
- Do not accept color differences among feathers as the whole answer; the logical failure is the change in meaning of *light*.

## Question 4: Part 2 Q2 – Star in space vs. star as celebrity (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**  
- **Canonical shifting term:** *star*.
- **Canonical meanings:** (1) an astronomical/celestial object such as a luminous ball of gas in space; (2) a famous, celebrated, or leading performer.
- **Canonical failure explanation:** Calling the singer a celebrity does not classify that person as an astronomical star, so the premise about celestial stars cannot be applied to the singer.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “sun-like object/celestial body” versus “celebrity/famous singer/performing star,” with any explanation that clearly identifies the category switch.
- **Required meaning:** The response must identify the term, both contextual meanings, and why the celebrity sense does not support the physical-space conclusion.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Correctly identifies *star*, states both meanings, and explains the invalid transfer between them
- 1 pt: Correctly identifies the term and both meanings but does not explain the failed inference; or identifies the term and failure while leaving one meaning only implied
- 0 pts: Gives one or fewer required components, treats a celebrity as literally an astronomical star, or gives an unrelated objection

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require “celestial body,” “ball of gas,” or any astronomy detail beyond a clear distinction between a space object and a famous performer.
- Do not withhold credit because a student calls the second use metaphorical; that language is acceptable if the two senses and inferential failure remain clear.

## Question 5: Part 3 Q1 – Fix end (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**  
- **Canonical key term:** *end*.
- **Canonical first meaning:** Goal, purpose, completion, fulfillment, or perfection—the sense in “the end of a thing is its perfection.”
- **Canonical second meaning:** Termination, stopping point, or cessation—the sense in “death is the end of life.”
- **Canonical follow judgment:** No; after the senses are fixed, life’s termination is not thereby its purpose, fulfillment, or perfection.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “aim” versus “finish,” “purpose” versus “termination,” or “what a thing is for” versus “when it stops.” Accept “does not follow,” “invalid because *end* changes meaning,” or a clear equivalent negative judgment.
- **Required meaning:** The response must disambiguate both uses of *end* and state that the conclusion does not follow. The prompt does not require a separate extended explanation once the two senses are clearly fixed.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Identifies *end*, clearly states both senses, and correctly says the conclusion does not follow
- 1 pt: Correctly distinguishes both senses but omits or obscures the follow judgment; or gives a correct negative judgment while leaving one sense vague
- 0 pts: Fails to distinguish the two senses, says the conclusion follows because death is literally an ending, or gives one or fewer required components

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the exact word “perfection” in the first definition; “purpose,” “goal,” or “fulfillment” preserves the relevant sense.
- Do not require a formal fallacy label or a paragraph explaining the failure.
- Do not accept “death completes life” as automatically equivalent to “death is life’s perfection”; “completion” must be clarified as purpose/fulfillment rather than mere chronological stopping.

## Question 6: Part 4 Q1 – Nobody paragraph (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**  
- **Canonical shifting expression:** *nobody*.
- **Canonical meanings:** (1) no person/not anybody in the general claim “nobody is perfect”; (2) an unimportant, unnoticed, or socially insignificant person in Jordan’s statement that he felt “like a nobody.”
- **Canonical failure explanation:** The flyer treats Jordan’s figurative description as though he were the quantifier “no person,” then combines it with “no person is perfect” to claim that Jordan must be perfect. The two senses do not establish that conclusion.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “not a single person” versus “someone who feels unimportant/ignored/unknown,” and explanations such as “the argument turns *nobody* into a person’s identity” or “it switches from no one to an insignificant person.”
- **Required meaning:** The response must identify the equivocation, distinguish the two uses, and explain why the shift makes the inference unreliable.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Identifies *nobody*, states both contextual senses, and explains how switching between them produces the unsupported perfection claim
- 1 pt: Identifies the term and both senses but gives little or no failure explanation; or recognizes the pun/bad inference while leaving one sense vague
- 0 pts: Gives one or fewer required components, treats “a nobody” as literally meaning no person exists, or merely agrees/disagrees with the flyer without analyzing the shift

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require terms such as “quantifier,” “figurative,” or “equivocation” if the student clearly explains the two meanings and shift.
- Do not require students to discuss the yearbook editor or other scene details.
- “Nobody is perfect, and Jordan is somebody” is not by itself a complete answer; it must identify how *nobody* changes from “no one” to “an unimportant person.”

## Question 7: Part 5 Q1 – Review: mayor tenure and lawful plan (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**  
- **Canonical conclusion:** The mayor’s new zoning plan must be lawful.
- **Canonical stated reason:** The mayor has served in office longer than any other council member.
- **Canonical hidden assumption:** Greater or longest tenure guarantees that the mayor’s policy is lawful, legally correct, or compliant with the law.
- **Canonical follow judgment:** No; length of service does not by itself establish a plan’s legality.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “the zoning plan follows the law/is legal” for the conclusion, “the mayor is the longest-serving person on the council” for the reason, assumptions such as “experience ensures legal decisions,” and “not necessarily/insufficient evidence” for the negative judgment.
- **Required meaning:** All four elements of the Week 1 test must be explicit and preserve the gap between tenure and legality.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Correctly supplies all four elements; labels and wording may vary if the roles are unambiguous
- 0 pts: Omits any requested element, swaps conclusion and reason, or says tenure proves lawfulness without recognizing the gap

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require a fallacy label or extended explanation.
- “The plan is good” is not equivalent to the conclusion; goodness and lawfulness are different claims.
- A bare “no” supplies only the follow judgment, not the other three required elements.

## Question 8: Part 5 Q2 – Review: satisfaction and scientific advice (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**  
- **Canonical conclusion:** The clinic’s medical advice must be scientifically correct.
- **Canonical stated reason:** The clinic received the highest patient-satisfaction scores.
- **Canonical hidden assumption:** High or highest patient satisfaction guarantees that medical advice is scientifically accurate.
- **Canonical follow judgment:** No; satisfaction measures patients’ reactions, not by itself the scientific correctness of the advice.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “the advice must be medically/scientifically accurate” for the conclusion, “patients rated the clinic most highly/were most satisfied” for the reason, assumptions such as “happy patients prove sound science,” and “not necessarily/insufficient evidence” for the negative judgment.
- **Required meaning:** All four elements must be explicit and preserve the distinction between satisfaction and scientific accuracy.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Correctly supplies all four elements; labels and sentence order may vary if the logical roles remain clear
- 0 pts: Omits any requested element, swaps roles, or treats satisfaction as proof of scientific correctness without recognizing the unsupported bridge

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the exact phrase “patient satisfaction” if the response clearly refers to the clinic’s highest ratings from patients.
- “The clinic is popular” may paraphrase the evidence only if tied to the stated highest satisfaction scores; popularity alone is less precise than the worksheet.
- A response that discusses good customer service but never identifies the scientific-accuracy conclusion is incomplete.

## Question 9: Part 5 Q3 – Review: hiring paragraph (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**  
- **Canonical conclusion:** The finalist should be hired.
- **Canonical stated support:** The finalist answered every technical question correctly; no other candidate completed the same skills trial; and the official note explicitly claims that this proven test performance guarantees long-term success in the role. A concise response may combine these as “the finalist uniquely performed perfectly on the skills test, which the panel says guarantees long-term success.”
- **Canonical hidden assumption:** Performance on this limited skills trial is enough to predict success across the full job and to make this finalist the appropriate hire; no other relevant qualifications or comparisons are needed.
- **Canonical follow judgment:** No or only partly. The test performance is relevant evidence, but it does not guarantee long-term job success, and the paragraph gives incomplete comparative information because the other candidates did not complete the same trial.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept any accurate paraphrase of the hiring conclusion and at least one central, non-distractor support statement. Accept hidden assumptions focused on the trial’s predictive power, the sufficiency of technical answers for the whole role, or the idea that test success alone settles hiring. Accept “not necessarily,” “partly supported,” or “the evidence supports consideration but not a guarantee” when the limitation is explained.
- **Required meaning:** The response must identify the recommendation to hire as the conclusion, use genuine hiring/test evidence rather than scene details, state an unstated bridge from trial performance to the hiring decision, and evaluate the strength of that bridge.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Correctly supplies all four elements, uses at least one genuine test/hiring support statement, gives a relevant hidden assumption, and judges the conclusion no/partly with a clear limitation
- 1 pt: Correctly identifies the hiring conclusion and genuine support but omits or weakly states the hidden assumption/follow judgment; or includes a distractor while still parsing most of the core argument correctly
- 0 pts: Misses the hiring conclusion, gives no genuine support, treats lunch/courtyard/cousin/meme details as the main reason, or merely summarizes the paragraph

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require every support detail; the prompt asks students to parse the main argument, and one accurately identified central reason can satisfy the stated-support element.
- The sentence that test performance “guarantees long-term success” is **explicit stated support**, not itself the hidden assumption. A hidden assumption must supply a further unstated bridge, such as treating this trial as sufficient evidence for the whole job or treating predicted success as enough to settle the hiring choice.
- Do not require exact wording or a specific order of the four elements.
- **Distractors:** lunch in the courtyard, rearranged chairs, the cousin’s dislike of early interviews, and the online meme.
