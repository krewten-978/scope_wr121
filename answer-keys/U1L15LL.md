# U1L15LL – Logic Lab Answer Key & Rubric

**Total Points:** 16

**Paired worksheet:** `graded-assignments/U1L15LL/worksheet.tex` / `.pdf` (Assignment ID **U1L15LL**).

**Paired reading:** Week 15 — verbal disputes, real disputes, mixed disputes, contested terms, definitions, shared claims, remaining disagreement, and dispute maps.

## Grading Vocabulary for This Key

- **Verbal dispute:** a disagreement caused mainly by different uses of a word or phrase.
- **Real dispute:** a disagreement that remains even after the key terms are clarified.
- **Mixed dispute:** a disagreement involving both word-confusion and real disagreement underneath.
- **Contested term:** the word whose meaning is part of the disagreement.
- **Shared claim:** something both sides would accept after the term is clarified.
- **Remaining disagreement:** what still must be argued after clarification.
- **Dispute map:** disputed claim; contested term; Side A meaning; Side B meaning; shared claim; remaining disagreement; classification.
- **Review terms:** prior-week discovery/proof/hypothesis tool and Week 13 source credibility/corroboration test.

Week 15 rewards reducing confusion without pretending every disagreement vanishes after a definition. Grade map and clarification *jobs*, not only formal labels, unless the prompt asks students to classify as verbal, real, or mixed.

## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Finished project (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Contested term: **finished**. Side A: every slide exists / the basic product has been created. Side B: the project is fully ready — sources checked and speaking notes practiced.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Term: “finished,” “what ‘finished’ means,” “done / complete.” Side A: slides made, product created, draft exists, surface completion. Side B: fully ready to present, quality-checked, practiced, sources verified. Ordinary paraphrases of both senses are fine.
- **Required meaning:** Name the contested word (or clear equivalent) and give **two distinct senses** matching the prompt’s sides. Do not require the label “contested term.” Do not require choosing which side is right.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Identifies the contested term and both meanings accurately
- 1 pt: Identifies the term but gives only one meaning, or both meanings are vague
- 0 pts: Treats one side as simply wrong without clarifying the term, wrong term, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the formal phrase “contested term” if “finished” and two senses are clear.
- Do not require not choosing a winner as a separate scored element when both meanings are clear.
- Do not accept a single blended meaning that collapses both sides.

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – Expensive trip (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Contested term: **expensive**. Side A: high total cost / high absolute price. Side B: too costly relative to this month’s budget / unaffordable under current constraints. Both can be true in different senses.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Term: “expensive,” “cost,” “affordable vs expensive.” Side A: high sticker price, a lot of money overall. Side B: not affordable this month, stretches the budget, relative cost. Accept that a moderate absolute price can still be “expensive” under a tight budget.
- **Required meaning:** Contested word plus two senses (absolute cost vs budget-relative cost). Settling whether the trip “is expensive” without the two senses fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Identifies the term and both meanings
- 1 pt: Identifies cost disagreement but not the two senses
- 0 pts: Only states whether the trip is expensive, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require saying “both can be true” if the two senses are clear.
- Do not require formal economics vocabulary.
- Ordinary language is fine.

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – Successful practice (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Classification: **mixed** (or verbal with a real standard-fight underneath). Contested term: **successful**. Coach: successful by attendance. Captain: successful by improvement in weak plays. Clarification needed: which standard (attendance, skill improvement, or both) is being used, and whether they still disagree about which standard matters most after defining the word.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Classification: “mixed,” “mostly verbal then real,” “verbal dispute about ‘successful’ that may leave a real coaching dispute.” Clarification: define successful / agree the standard / separate attendance from improvement. Do not require the exact word “mixed” if the student clearly separates word-confusion from a remaining standard fight and classifies consistently with that analysis.
- **Required meaning:** Plausible classification tied to two standards for “successful,” plus what must be clarified. Choosing coach or captain as simply right without dispute analysis fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Classifies plausibly and explains the needed clarification
- 1 pt: Names the term or one standard but weak classification/clarification
- 0 pts: Chooses a side without dispute analysis, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Accept verbal, mixed, or carefully worded “verbal with remaining real disagreement” when supported.
- Do not require predicting whether they will still disagree after clarification.
- Do not require formal labels if the two standards and clarification need are clear **and** the prompt’s classify request is answered with ordinary language that matches verbal/real/mixed distinctions.

## Question 4: Part 2 Q2 – Speed bumps and safety (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Classification: **real** dispute. Both sides already share a definition of safe street (fewer crashes and fewer serious injuries). Evidence that matters: crash data, injury records, before/after comparisons, traffic speed, near-miss reports, or similar relevant outcome data.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Classification: “real,” “about the facts/evidence,” “not mainly verbal.” Evidence: any one or two relevant empirical indicators (crashes, injuries, speeds, hospital data, before/after study). Ordinary language is fine.
- **Required meaning:** Real (shared definition; remaining fight is factual/evidential) **and** at least one type of relevant evidence. Treating it as merely verbal despite the shared definition fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Classifies as real and names relevant evidence
- 1 pt: Says real dispute but gives little or no evidence detail, or names evidence without clear real classification
- 0 pts: Treats it as merely verbal despite shared definition, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the model’s full evidence checklist; one well-aimed evidence type is enough for full credit.
- Do not require students to decide who is right about the bumps.
- Formal word “real” is preferred because the prompt asks to classify; accept clear equivalents such as “factual dispute after shared definition.”

## Question 5: Part 3 Q1 – Committed member (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Shared claim after clarification: attendance and completing work are both signs of commitment, **or** this member attends meetings but may not complete between-meeting work, **or** both sides care about commitment defined somehow. Remaining disagreement: which standard should count more, whether this member meets the relevant standard, or whether commitment requires both presence and follow-through.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Shared: both value commitment; member shows up; work completion matters; two different tests of commitment. Remaining: which test rules, does this person qualify, must both conditions be met, how to weigh attendance vs homework. Compact answers are fine.
- **Required meaning:** Prompt asks for **both** a shared claim that may emerge **and** the remaining disagreement. Only one of the two is partial.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Names a plausible shared claim and remaining disagreement
- 1 pt: Gives only shared claim or only remaining disagreement
- 0 pts: Gives no clarification of the dispute, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the exact shared claim in the model; any defensible post-clarification agreement plus a real remaining fight earns full credit.
- Do not require settling which standard is correct.
- Do not require formal map labels beyond what the prompt names.

## Question 6: Part 3 Q2 – Original essay (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Clarification may settle that “original” can mean not plagiarized at the sentence level **or** intellectually fresh in argument; sides may agree the essay uses no copied sentences. What still needs judgment: whether the argument itself is fresh/independent or merely rearranges common points.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Settled by clarification: no-copying sense, plagiarism vs freshness distinction, sentence-level originality. Still needs judgment: idea-level originality, independent thinking, whether rearranging common points counts, quality of the argument. Ordinary language is fine.
- **Required meaning:** Separate what clarification can settle from what still needs judgment. Treating originality as one obvious meaning without analysis fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Separates what clarification settles from what still needs judgment
- 1 pt: Mentions plagiarism or fresh argument only
- 0 pts: Treats originality as one obvious meaning without analysis, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require both possible senses of “original” as long as the settle-vs-still-judge split is clear.
- Do not require a verdict on whether the essay is original.
- Accept “anti-plagiarism vs intellectual freshness” paraphrases.

## Question 7: Part 4 Q1 – Lunch policy dispute map (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Disputed claim: whether the lunch policy is equal. Contested term: **equal**. Side A: same written thirty-minute period for every student. Side B: comparable actual eating time / practical access. Shared claim: everyone receives thirty minutes on the written schedule, and actual eating time matters. Remaining disagreement: whether formal sameness is enough, whether location/walking differences make the policy unequal, and what adjustment is required. Classification: **mixed** — clarifying “equal” helps, but a real policy disagreement remains.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Ordinary-language map labels (disagreement / word in dispute / Group A means / Group B means / agree that / still fight over / type of dispute). Shared claim may combine or slightly rephrase the paragraph’s two agreements. Remaining disagreement may focus on formal vs practical equality or on remedy. Classification: mixed preferred; carefully justified real-with-prior-verbal-work can earn full credit if remaining policy fight is clear.
- **Required meaning:** Prompt lists seven map jobs: disputed claim, contested term, Side A meaning, Side B meaning, shared claim, remaining disagreement, classification. Grade those **jobs**, not exact model wording. One-side cheerleading without mapping fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Completes the map with classification and remaining disagreement (all listed jobs present in substance)
- 1 pt: Includes some map elements but incomplete (missing two or more jobs, or classification without support)
- 0 pts: Accepts one side without dispute mapping, plot-like restatement only, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the model’s full inventory of remaining-disagreement bullets; one clear remaining policy fight is enough if other map jobs are present.
- Do not require identical labels if contents match the seven jobs.
- Do not require quotation from the paragraph.
- Compact bullet or paragraph form is fine.

## Question 8: Part 5 Q1 – Review: courtyard hypothesis (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Hypothesis: the drain is clogged. What must be discovered: whether the courtyard actually floods and whether the drain is clogged (observation, inspection, records, or testimony). Proof would require evidence connecting the clogged drain to the flooding, not merely imagining the explanation.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Hypothesis: “guess that the drain is clogged,” “possible explanation,” “imagined cause.” Discovery: check flooding, inspect drain, look at the courtyard, ask witnesses, maintenance records. Proof: evidence linking clog to flood, tested explanation, not imagination alone.
- **Required meaning:** All three jobs from the prompt: hypothesis, needed discovery, and what would count as proof. Treating the imagined explanation as already proved fails.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Identifies hypothesis, needed discovery, and proof requirement
- 0 pts: Treats the imagined explanation as proof, misses the three-way split, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the formal words “hypothesis,” “discovery,” and “proof” if the three roles are clear.
- Do not require a full experimental design; naming observation/inspection plus link-to-flood is enough.
- One-point item: all three elements needed for the point; no half points.

## Question 9: Part 5 Q2 – Review: celebrity endorsement source (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Corroboration is weak because three advertisements may repeat the same non-independent celebrity endorsement. Better evidence: independent reviews, user results, teacher evidence, learning research, expert evaluation, or transparent performance data.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Weakness: same source repeated, ads not independent, celebrity not relevant expertise, paid endorsement, echo not corroboration. Better evidence: any one or two independent, relevant sources (tests, teachers, studies, unbiased users, data).
- **Required meaning:** Explain shared-source / irrelevant-authority weakness **and** name more relevant evidence. Counting repeated ads as independent proof fails.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Explains shared-source/irrelevant-authority weakness and names better evidence
- 0 pts: Counts repeated ads as independent proof, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the model’s full better-evidence list; one well-aimed independent source type is enough.
- Do not require formal “corroboration” if non-independence is clear.
- One-point item: both weakness and better evidence needed for the point.

**Overall Notes for Grading This Lab:**
- Whole points only. Total: 2+2+2+2+2+2+2+1+1 = 16 points.
- Credit students who reduce confusion without pretending that every disagreement disappears after definition.
- Grade dispute-map and clarification contents over exclusive model wording.
