# U1L17LE – Lit Example Worksheet Answer Key & Rubric

**Total Points:** 16

**Paired reader:** `units/elements-of-logic-week17/lit-examples/U1L17LE_lit_example_reader.tex` / `.pdf`.

**Paired worksheet:** `graded-assignments/U1L17LE/worksheet.tex` / `.pdf` (Assignment ID **U1L17LE**).

**Purpose:** Students apply Week 17 multi-tool self-deception autopsy to Macbeth’s private reasoning before Duncan’s murder and Brutus’s orchard justification of preventive killing.

## Grading Vocabulary for This Key

- **Self-deception autopsy:** desire; conclusion; stated reasons; hidden assumption; exposing tool; remaining need; repaired claim.
- **Private argument / public cover:** inner case versus cleaner civic or moral story.
- **Motivated reasoning:** desire-first conclusion dressed as necessity.
- **Exposing tool:** any prior Whately habit (hidden assumption, analogy test, missing proof, term shift, rhetoric-vs-proof, source test, etc.).

Plot summary alone does not earn points. Quotation is optional unless needed for clarity. Credit multi-tool readings; do not require one official tool name if the diagnostic work is precise. Passage-grounded alternatives that preserve desire-first structure, unproved necessity, and repaired narrower claims earn full credit.

## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Macbeth desire and conclusion (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Desire/feared loss: kingship/success without consequence; fear that the act will not be “the be-all and the end-all.” Conclusion being tested: whether to assassinate Duncan / whether the deed can be done cleanly and finally.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Desire: crown, power, success, secure the murder’s payoff, fear of consequences/aftermath. Conclusion under test: kill Duncan or not; can the deed end the problem; is the murder doable as a clean final act. Ordinary paraphrases fine.
- **Required meaning:** Desire or feared loss **and** the conclusion under test. Plot only fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Names desire-pressure and the conclusion under test
- 1 pt: Thin or partial
- 0 pts: Plot only, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the exact “be-all and end-all” quotation.
- Do not require both desire and fear labels; one clear driver plus the tested conclusion is enough.
- Do not require deciding that he has already decided to kill.

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – Reasons against and spur (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Against: double trust — kinsman, subject, and host duties; he should shut the door against a murderer, not bear the knife. Spur named: vaulting ambition, with no other spur to prick the sides of his intent.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Against: kinship, loyalty as subject, hospitality/host duty, should protect Duncan not kill him, multiple trust bonds. Spur: ambition, vaulting ambition, desire for the crown. Both sides required.
- **Required meaning:** Reasons against the murder **and** the spur he names. Plot summary without both jobs fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Duties against and ambition spur both clear
- 1 pt: One side only
- 0 pts: Plot summary, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require listing all three trust roles if double/multiple trust or host-kin-subject idea is clear.
- Do not require the exact “vaulting ambition” phrase if ambition is clearly the named spur.
- Quotation optional.

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – Hidden assumption and tool (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Possible hidden assumption: if consequences could be trapped/ended here, the murder would be acceptable; or ambition plus opportunity can outweigh trust duties. Exposing tools for later self-deception risk: hidden-assumption test, rhetoric-vs-proof (manhood shame), source test (witches/apparitions), equivocation on safety/fate, form-vs-fact. Strong answers name a real assumption and a fitting tool.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Assumptions: clean ending would justify murder; success erases moral problem; duties don’t bind if ambition is strong; if I can trap consequences, the deed is OK. Tools: any fitting prior Whately habit named or clearly described. One assumption + one tool.
- **Required meaning:** Hidden assumption needed to treat murder as acceptable **and** one exposing tool for later self-deception risk. No analytical content fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Plausible assumption plus fitting tool
- 1 pt: One element only
- 0 pts: No analytical content, plot only, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require one official tool from a closed list.
- Do not require predicting Lady Macbeth’s later pressure if another fitting tool is named.
- Ordinary language for the assumption is fine.

## Question 4: Part 2 Q2 – Repaired Macbeth claim (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** A repaired narrower claim: I am ambitious and tempted; I know kinship, loyalty, and hospitality count against murdering Duncan; wanting the crown does not make the murder necessary or justified.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Any honest narrower claim that (a) admits desire/temptation, (b) keeps the trust/moral reasons in view, and (c) refuses false necessity (“must kill” / “ambition justifies it”). Examples: I want the crown but duties still bind; temptation is not proof; I see reasons against and have not justified the act.
- **Required meaning:** Narrower honest claim without false necessity. Restating that he should kill, or only “don’t do it” without claim craft, fails full credit.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Narrower honest claim without false necessity
- 1 pt: Partially repaired
- 0 pts: Restates that he should kill, or merely “don’t do it” without claim craft, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the model’s exact sentence.
- Do not require listing all three duties if moral constraints remain clear.
- First-person or third-person repaired claims both OK.

## Question 5: Part 3 Q1 – Brutus desire and conclusion (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Desire: protect Rome/the general good; preserve republican liberty; maintain his honorable identity as Rome’s guardian. Conclusion: Caesar must die (“It must be by his death”).
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Desire: Rome’s good, liberty, honor, prevent tyranny, public virtue. Conclusion: Caesar must die; kill Caesar; preventive death is required. Ordinary paraphrases fine.
- **Required meaning:** Desire for Rome and/or honor **and** the death conclusion. Plot only fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Desire/honor-aim and death conclusion
- 1 pt: One element
- 0 pts: Plot only, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require both Rome-good and personal honor if one clear driver plus the death conclusion is present.
- Do not require the exact “must be by his death” quotation.
- Do not require condemning Brutus as merely vain.

## Question 6: Part 3 Q2 – Reasons, analogy, unproved (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Stated reasons: no personal cause; for the general; crown may change nature. Analogy: serpent’s egg — kill in the shell before mischief. Unproved: that crowning will necessarily change Caesar into a mischievous tyrant; that death is required rather than lesser restraints; that analogy of fixed animal kind fits a human political actor.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Reasons/analogy: general good not personal grudge; crown changes nature; serpent-egg preventive analogy. Unproved: future tyranny not certain; death not only option; analogy overextends; necessity not shown. Reasons + analogy cluster and clear unproved burden both needed.
- **Required meaning:** Stated reasons and analogy **and** what remains unproved. “Brutus is noble/wrong” alone fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Reasons/analogy and clear unproved burden
- 1 pt: Partial
- 0 pts: Says only Brutus is noble or wrong, plot only, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require every model reason if the preventive-analogy structure and an unproved burden are clear.
- Do not require formal “disanalogy” if the unproved leap is explained.
- One clear unproved burden is enough for that job.

## Question 7: Part 4 Q1 – Brutus full autopsy (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Desire: Rome’s general good / honorable preventive guardianship. Conclusion: Caesar must die. Stated reasons: crown danger; general not personal cause; serpent-egg analogy. Hidden assumption: future nature-change is sure enough to require present killing; analogy’s relevant similarity holds. Exposing tool: analogy/disanalogy, hidden assumption, missing proof, or public-cover of necessity. Remaining need: evidence of tyranny trajectory, limits of preventive killing, political alternatives. Repaired claim: Caesar’s possible crowning worries me for Rome; that fear alone does not prove he must be killed in the shell.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Ordinary-language autopsy covering the seven prompt jobs. Fitting tool names may vary. Repaired claim must drop false necessity while allowing honest public worry.
- **Required meaning:** Seven autopsy jobs in substance. Plot summary fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Completes coherent autopsy
- 1 pt: Incomplete map
- 0 pts: Plot summary, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the model’s full remaining-need checklist; one clear remaining need is enough with other jobs present.
- Do not require one official tool name if the diagnosis is precise.
- Compact bullets or paragraph form is fine.

## Question 8: Part 4 Q2 – Comparison (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Both speakers can see pieces of truth—Macbeth names duties and ambition; Brutus names public worry—yet desire recruits reasons toward a wanted act. Logical forms appear (duties listed; analogy offered) while necessity is overclaimed. Private reasoning hides by dressing want as must.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Any comparison of **both** characters showing (a) recognizable logical forms or partial insight and (b) self-hiding / desire-first necessity. Uneven length OK if both appear. Tools may be named differently.
- **Required meaning:** Both Macbeth and Brutus + how private reasoning can hide while still using logical forms. Plot only fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Compares both with tool-aware explanation
- 1 pt: One character well or thin compare
- 0 pts: Plot only, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require equal paragraph length.
- Do not require identical model themes if want-as-must / forms-without-proof is clear for both.
- Do not accept cynicism that “all reasons are fake” as a substitute for the comparison structure.

**Overall Notes for Grading This Worksheet:**
- Whole points only. Total: 8 × 2 = 16 points.
- Credit multi-tool readings; do not require one official tool name if the diagnostic work is precise.
- Plot summary alone never earns points.
