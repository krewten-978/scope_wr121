## Grading Vocabulary for This Key

- **Either/or (disjunction):** a claim offering alternatives.
- **Disjunctive syllogism:** from either A or B, deny one branch and keep the other (when the premises are granted).
- **False choice:** an either/or that pretends two options exhaust the live field when they do not.
- **Antecedent:** the if-part of a conditional.
- **Consequent:** the then-part of a conditional.
- **Affirming the antecedent:** grant A; conclude B (valid if the conditional is granted).
- **Denying the consequent:** deny B; conclude not-A (valid if the conditional is granted).
- **Affirming the consequent:** grant B; conclude A (invalid as a proof of A).
- **Denying the antecedent:** deny A; conclude not-B (invalid as a proof that B fails).
- **Form test:** if the premises are granted, does the conclusion follow?
- **Fact test:** are the premises true, justified, or still doubtful?

Accept ordinary-language equivalents. Do not require Latin names (modus ponens / tollens) unless the student uses them correctly.

## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Science fair disjunctive syllogism (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Either/or claim:** Either the science fair is moved to the gym, or it is canceled.
- **Denied branch:** It is not moved to the gym.
- **Conclusion:** It is canceled.
- **Follows?** Yes, if the premises are granted (disjunctive syllogism).

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Correctly names either/or, denied branch, and conclusion, and judges that the conclusion follows if premises are granted
- 1 pt: Gets the map mostly right but misses the follow judgment, or judges follows correctly but mangles one branch label
- 0 pts: Says it does not follow without explaining a real defect, confuses branches, or gives no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:** Do not require the phrase “disjunctive syllogism.” “Yes, if we grant only those two options and the denial” is enough.

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – Bus leave invalid or-move (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
**No; the conclusion does not follow.** From “Either the bus leaves at 3:10, or late riders will miss the meet” and “late riders will miss the meet,” it does not follow that the bus does not leave at 3:10. Missing the meet could be true for other reasons, and affirming one branch of an either/or does not by itself deny the other unless exclusivity has been established.

Accept: “invalid,” “does not follow,” “affirming one side doesn’t eliminate the other,” or a clear counterexample.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Correct invalid/no judgment with a relevant reason about branches or alternative explanations
- 1 pt: Correct invalid judgment with thin or no reason, or a partly accurate reason without a clear judgment
- 0 pts: Says it follows, or gives an unrelated complaint

**Notes for grading:** Do not require the technical label “affirming a disjunct.” Ordinary language is enough.

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – Overnight retreat false choice (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Stated options:** sign up for the overnight leadership retreat, or do not care about the school.
- **Ignored live options (any one):** care about the school but oppose the retreat for safety, cost, family, academics, accessibility, timing, or a better local alternative; support school spirit without this particular overnight event; want more information before signing.
- **Why unfair:** caring about the school is not exhausted by one signup; the either/or forces a loyalty verdict without proving that non-signup equals not caring.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Names both stated options, gives at least one concrete omitted live option, and explains why the proof is unfair
- 1 pt: Sees false-choice pressure but omits either a stated-option map or a concrete omitted option
- 0 pts: Merely agrees/disagrees with retreats, or gives no compound diagnosis

**Notes for grading:** Do not require the exact phrase “false choice.” “Stacked either/or,” “fake dilemma,” or “only two options when there are more” are acceptable.

## Question 4: Part 3 Q1 – Fire alarm affirming antecedent (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Antecedent:** the fire alarm is a real emergency.
- **Consequent:** students must exit to the north lot.
- **Follows?** Yes, if both premises are granted (affirm the antecedent / if-granted-then-follow).

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Correct antecedent and consequent labels (or clear equivalent) and a yes/follows judgment
- 1 pt: Labels mostly right but follow judgment missing/wrong, or follow judgment right with swapped labels that still show the link

**Notes for grading:** Accept “if-part / then-part.” Do not require “modus ponens.”

## Question 5: Part 3 Q2 – Lobby light denying consequent (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
**Yes; it follows if the premises are granted.** If unlocked doors require the light strip on, and the light strip is not on, then the doors are not unlocked. This denies the consequent and rejects the antecedent.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Yes/follows with a brief accurate explanation of the conditional link
- 1 pt: Correct judgment with no real explanation, or explanation partly confused but judgment right
- 0 pts: Says it does not follow, or confuses this with affirming the consequent

**Notes for grading:** Do not require “modus tollens.” “If the rule is true and the result is missing, the condition fails” is enough.

## Question 6: Part 4 Q1 – Chess club affirming the consequent (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
**No; the conclusion does not follow.** This affirms the consequent: being allowed to use Room 214 does not prove the club is school-sponsored, because other reasons could explain room access. Plain-English pattern names: “backwards if/then,” “affirming the then-part,” or “treating the result as proof of the condition.”

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Invalid/no judgment plus a clear pattern description or counter-reason
- 1 pt: Invalid judgment only, or pattern named without a clear judgment
- 0 pts: Says it follows, or only discusses whether chess is good

**Notes for grading:** Accept any clear ordinary-language description of affirming the consequent. Exact technical name not required.

## Question 7: Part 4 Q2 – Trip approval denying antecedent + fact (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
**Form:** The conclusion does **not** follow. From “If every senior submitted, then the trip is approved” and “not every senior submitted,” it does not follow that the trip is not approved (denying the antecedent). The trip might still be approved by exception, late waiver, majority rule, or another policy path.

**Fact / soundness note (any one relevant):** Even a repaired form would still need proof of the actual approval rule, whether the universal Friday rule is the real policy, whether exceptions exist, and whether “approved” means final transportation/funding approval. Valid form alone never settles policy facts.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Correct invalid form judgment with a relevant reason, plus at least one concrete residual fact/proof need
- 1 pt: Handles either the form defect or the residual fact need well, but not both
- 0 pts: Says the printed argument is valid and sound as written, or gives no relevant analysis

**Notes for grading:** Do not require the phrase “denying the antecedent.” Accept “denying the if-part doesn’t kill the then-part.”

## Question 8: Part 5 Q1 – Review: songbirds form/fact (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
**Form:** valid (if premises granted, conclusion follows). **Fact problem:** at least one premise is false/doubtful (not all songbirds fly long open-water distances; and/or robins are not the kind of bird that fits a premise built for long open-water flight as printed). Main fact failure is enough if form is marked valid.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Valid form + names a relevant fact problem
- 0 pts: Calls form invalid, or only discusses birds with no form/fact split

**Notes for grading:** One clear false/doubtful premise is enough. Do not require both premises.
