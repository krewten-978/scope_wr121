## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Championship from three wins (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**  
- **Canonical conclusion:** The team will definitely win the championship.
- **Canonical stated reason:** The team has won its last three games.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept accurate paraphrases such as “they are certain/sure to win the championship” for the conclusion and “they won three games in a row/their three most recent games” for the reason.
- **Required meaning:** The response must identify the predicted championship win as the claim being supported and the three recent wins as the support actually stated.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Identifies both logical roles with the required meaning; labels or sentence order may vary if it remains clear which statement is the conclusion and which is the reason
- 0 pts: Omits either requested element, swaps the roles, or substitutes a different claim or reason

**Notes for grading:**  
- Do not require the exact terms “conclusion” and “stated reason” if the two roles are otherwise unambiguous.
- Do not require a hidden assumption or a judgment about whether the conclusion follows; the prompt asks only for the claim and reason.
- Do not accept “the team is playing well” as the stated reason unless the response also identifies the actual fact of three recent wins.

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – Sincere apology and regret (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**  
- **Canonical conclusion:** She must truly regret what she did.
- **Canonical stated reason:** She apologized in a sincere tone.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “she really/genuinely feels sorry or remorseful” for the conclusion and “her apology sounded sincere/genuine” for the reason.
- **Required meaning:** The response must identify genuine regret as the claim and the sincere-sounding apology as the support actually given.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Identifies both requested elements with their logical roles clear
- 0 pts: Omits either element, swaps the roles, or gives a materially different claim or reason

**Notes for grading:**  
- Do not require the words “regret,” “conclusion,” or “stated reason” when an unambiguous paraphrase preserves the meaning and roles.
- Do not award credit for merely saying “she apologized”; the stated reason includes that the apology was delivered in a sincere tone.
- Do not require an explanation of whether sincere tone proves regret; that task was not asked here.

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – Study app downloads (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**  
- **Canonical conclusion:** The app must be the best tool for learning chemistry.
- **Canonical stated reason:** It has been downloaded five million times.
- **Canonical hidden assumption:** A very high download count or popularity reliably establishes that an app is the best tool for learning chemistry.
- **Canonical follow judgment:** No; the conclusion does not follow because popularity or download volume does not by itself establish educational quality or comparative superiority.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “most useful/effective chemistry-learning app” for “best tool” only when the response preserves the claim of superiority. Accept hidden assumptions phrased as “popular means best,” “millions of users cannot be wrong,” or “downloads measure learning quality.” Accept “not necessarily,” “insufficient evidence,” or “the reason does not prove the claim” as a negative follow judgment.
- **Required meaning:** All four requested elements must be explicit, and the explanation must preserve the gap between popularity and being the best learning tool.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Supplies all four elements correctly; the negative follow judgment may be concise but must be clear
- 1 pt: Supplies exactly three correct elements, or identifies conclusion and reason correctly while giving a recognizably relevant but incomplete assumption/follow analysis
- 0 pts: Supplies two or fewer correct elements, reverses conclusion and reason, or claims that download count proves the app is best without identifying the logical gap

**Notes for grading:**  
- Do not require the model wording or a formal fallacy label.
- Do not treat “the app is good” as fully equivalent to the conclusion; “good” weakens the worksheet’s claim that it is the **best**.
- A bare “no” can satisfy the follow element because the prompt asks whether it follows, but it does not substitute for the separately requested hidden assumption.

## Question 4: Part 2 Q2 – Saturday rain and Friday concert (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**  
- **Canonical conclusion:** The Friday-night outdoor concert should be called off/canceled.
- **Canonical stated reason:** The weather report predicts rain on Saturday.
- **Canonical hidden assumption:** Saturday’s rain forecast is relevant to conditions at the Friday-night concert (for example, that the rain will begin early enough to affect Friday).
- **Canonical follow judgment:** No; as stated, a forecast for Saturday does not establish that Friday’s concert should be canceled.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “postpone/cancel the Friday concert” for the conclusion, “Saturday is forecast to be rainy” for the reason, and any hidden assumption that explicitly bridges the day mismatch. Accept “not necessarily,” “insufficient support,” or “the dates do not match” for the follow judgment.
- **Required meaning:** The response must provide all four requested elements and recognize that the stated forecast concerns a different day from the event.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Correctly supplies all four elements and makes the Friday/Saturday gap clear in either the assumption or follow judgment
- 1 pt: Correctly identifies conclusion and stated reason but gives an incomplete assumption or follow analysis, or supplies three correct elements without clearly explaining the day mismatch
- 0 pts: Supplies two or fewer correct elements, swaps the roles, or treats Saturday rain alone as sufficient proof that Friday’s event must be canceled

**Notes for grading:**  
- Do not require the exact phrase “day mismatch”; a clear statement that Saturday weather may not affect Friday is sufficient.
- Do not require the student to challenge the forecast’s accuracy. That can be a secondary concern, but a response that discusses only forecast reliability and never bridges the different days has not identified the central hidden assumption.
- A bare “no” may satisfy the follow element, but not the separately requested hidden-assumption element.

## Question 5: Part 3 Q1 – Unanimous council vote and fairness (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**  
- **Canonical conclusion:** The proposal must be fair.
- **Canonical stated reason:** Every member of the student council voted for the proposal.
- **Canonical hidden assumption:** Unanimous student-council approval guarantees that the proposal is fair.
- **Canonical follow judgment:** No; agreement by the council does not by itself prove fairness.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “the proposal treats people justly/equitably” for the conclusion, “the entire council supported it/no council member opposed it” for the reason, and assumptions such as “if everyone on the council agrees, the decision is fair.” Accept “not necessarily,” “insufficient evidence,” or “consensus does not equal fairness” for the follow judgment.
- **Required meaning:** All four requested elements must be explicit, and the response must distinguish the fact of unanimous agreement from the separate quality of fairness.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Correctly supplies all four elements and identifies the agreement-to-fairness gap
- 1 pt: Supplies three correct elements, or identifies conclusion and reason but gives a relevant yet incomplete assumption/follow analysis
- 0 pts: Supplies two or fewer correct elements, swaps the roles, or says unanimity proves fairness without recognizing the unsupported link

**Notes for grading:**  
- Do not require a fallacy name, the phrase “majority agreement,” or any particular sentence order.
- “Everyone voted for it, so it is popular” is not the worksheet’s conclusion; popularity and fairness are materially different claims.
- A response may criticize the council as biased or unrepresentative, but it must still identify the stated unanimous vote and the fairness claim to receive full credit.

## Question 6: Part 3 Q2 – Most sources read and correctness (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**  
- **Canonical conclusion:** Jordan’s conclusion must be correct.
- **Canonical stated reason:** No one in the debate club has read more sources than Jordan.
- **Canonical hidden assumption:** Reading at least as many sources as everyone else guarantees that one’s conclusion is correct.
- **Canonical follow judgment:** No; research quantity alone does not guarantee truth or sound interpretation.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “Jordan must be right” for the conclusion, “Jordan read the most sources/is tied for the greatest number read” for the reason, and assumptions such as “the best-read person is always correct” or “more sources ensure a true conclusion.” Accept “not necessarily,” “insufficient evidence,” or “the sources could be weak/misread” for the negative follow judgment.
- **Required meaning:** All four requested elements must be explicit and preserve the difference between volume of research and correctness.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Correctly supplies all four elements and identifies the research-quantity-to-truth gap
- 1 pt: Supplies three correct elements, or identifies conclusion and reason but gives a relevant yet incomplete assumption/follow analysis
- 0 pts: Supplies two or fewer correct elements, swaps the roles, or treats the number of sources as proof that Jordan is correct

**Notes for grading:**  
- Do not require the exact phrases “research volume” or “quantity of reading.”
- Because “no one has read more” permits a tie, accept “Jordan read the most or was tied for most”; do not require the stronger claim that Jordan alone read more than everyone else.
- Do not substitute the council-vote issue from the previous question; this item concerns research quantity, not group agreement.

## Question 7: Part 4 Q1 – Bus service paragraph (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**  
- **Canonical conclusion:** Late-night bus service should be expanded/widened across the east side.
- **Canonical stated support:** Evening-shift workers often cannot get home safely, and ridership on the pilot route increased after the trial began.
- **Canonical hidden assumption:** The safety need and pilot results are relevant to and representative of the proposed east-side expansion.
- **Canonical follow judgment:** No or only partly. The reasons support considering expanded late-night service, but the paragraph does not establish that the pilot and safety evidence specifically justify expansion across the whole east side.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** For the conclusion, accept “the city/council should broaden late-night east-side bus service.” Because the worksheet asks for a singular “stated reason” even though the paragraph supplies two supporting considerations, accept either the worker-safety reason, the increased pilot ridership, or a concise summary containing both. For the assumption, accept any clear bridge from the cited need or pilot evidence to east-side expansion. For the follow judgment, accept “not fully,” “not necessarily,” “partially supported,” or “the general expansion is supported but the east-side scope is not proved,” provided the response explains the evidential or geographic gap.
- **Required meaning:** The response must identify the final east-side expansion decision as the main conclusion, use at least one actual supporter statement as stated support, provide a relevant unstated bridge, and evaluate whether that support establishes the conclusion. Scene-setting details and jokes are not the argument’s main support.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Correct conclusion; at least one genuine stated supporting reason; a relevant hidden assumption; and a “no/partly/not fully” judgment that recognizes the gap between the evidence and the full east-side conclusion
- 1 pt: Identifies the main conclusion and at least one genuine supporting reason but omits or weakly states the hidden assumption or follow judgment; or includes a distractor while still correctly parsing most of the core argument
- 0 pts: Misses the main conclusion, gives no genuine supporting reason, or mostly summarizes scene details without parsing the requested logical roles

**Notes for grading:**  
- Do not require both supporter statements for full credit because the student-facing prompt asks for “stated reason” in the singular. Either worker safety or increased pilot ridership is sufficient if correctly identified; both may be summarized.
- Accept close paraphrases; do not require the council’s sentence word for word.
- **Do not accept as the main stated support:** the crowded hall, protesters, reporter, Ortiz’s brother’s missed work, budget charts, scooter joke, or the mere fact that the council voted. Ortiz’s anecdote is related to transit problems but is presented as scene detail, not one of the supporters’ stated reasons for the final east-side expansion.
- Do not award full credit for “yes, because the council voted” or for a bare “no/partly” with no hidden assumption or gap identified; the prompt separately requests all four elements.

**Overall Notes for Grading This Lab:**  
- Reward accurate logical roles over polished prose.  
- Whole points only (1, 2, 0). No half points.  
- Total: Part 1 Q1 (1) + Part 1 Q2 (1) + Part 2 Q1 (2) + Part 2 Q2 (2) + Part 3 Q1 (2) + Part 3 Q2 (2) + Part 4 Q1 (2) = 12 points.
