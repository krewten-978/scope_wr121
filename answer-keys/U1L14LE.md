# U1L14LE – Lit Example Worksheet Answer Key & Rubric

**Total Points:** 16

**Paired reader:** `units/elements-of-logic-week14/lit-examples/U1L14LE_lit_example_reader.tex` / `.pdf`.

**Paired worksheet:** `graded-assignments/U1L14LE/worksheet.tex` / `.pdf` (Assignment ID **U1L14LE**).

**Purpose:** Students apply Week 14 discovery / proof / judgment tools to Macbeth's late discoveries and Caesar's response to warnings and interpretations.

## Grading Vocabulary for This Key

- **Discovery:** learning a fact or meaning not previously known.
- **Proof:** showing that a conclusion follows from reasons or evidence.
- **Judgment:** deciding how to act or believe when evidence, interpretation, and uncertainty remain.
- **Clarified term/sign:** a word, phrase, sign, prophecy, or image whose meaning must be made precise before reasoning from it.
- **Inference test:** asking whether the conclusion actually follows from the evidence or statement.
- **Tool map:** question; needed discovery; clarified term or sign; inference test; judgment.

Plot summary alone does not earn points. Quotation is permitted but not required. Credit students who distinguish evidence from interpretation and who show that late discovery exposes earlier failures of judgment.

## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Macbeth's late facts (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Macbeth discovers that Birnam Wood can “move” because soldiers carry branches toward Dunsinane, and he discovers that Macduff was not born in the ordinary way but “from his mother's womb untimely ripp'd.” These facts reveal limits in Macbeth’s earlier understanding of the apparitions.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** (1) Birnam Wood moving = army with boughs/branches / camouflage march. (2) Macduff not “of woman born” in the ordinary sense = Caesarean / untimely ripped. Accept clear paraphrase without quotation.
- **Required meaning:** Both late discoveries must be identifiable. One discovery only is partial. Pure battle plot without these two fact-meanings fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Names both late discoveries accurately
- 1 pt: Names one discovery clearly, or both only vaguely
- 0 pts: Incorrect, plot summary only, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require exact Shakespearean wording.
- Do not require full moral analysis on this item.
- Do not require naming the apparitions again if the two facts are clear.

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – Macbeth's phrase judged too quickly (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Students may use either phrase. “Birnam Wood shall come” did not have to mean literal trees walking; it could be fulfilled by soldiers carrying branches. “None of woman born” did not cover Macduff in the way Macbeth assumed. Later events reveal that Macbeth treated ambiguous words as settled guarantees.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Phrase: Birnam Wood / moving forest; none of woman born / no man born of woman. Revealed meaning: branches/soldiers; Macduff’s untimely birth. Accept “he took the prophecy too literally” with a specific phrase.
- **Required meaning:** Identify a key phrase **and** explain the later-revealed meaning (or how the earlier reading failed). Saying only “the witches lied” or “Macbeth was unlucky” without term/meaning work fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Identifies a key phrase and explains the later revealed meaning
- 1 pt: Identifies a phrase but gives little explanation, or explains ambiguity without naming a phrase
- 0 pts: Says only that the witches lied or that Macbeth was unlucky, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Either major phrase is enough; students need not cover both.
- Do not require accusing the witches of lying; ambiguity/misreading is the course point.
- Ordinary paraphrase is enough.

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – Apparitions not proof of safety (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** The apparitions’ words were testimony from a dangerous and ambiguous source, not a complete proof of safety. Macbeth had not clarified the terms, tested the source’s reliability, or considered alternative meanings. One interpretation that favors his ambition does not prove the conclusion “I am safe.”
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “words were ambiguous,” “source untrustworthy / tempting,” “safety doesn’t follow,” “he didn’t clarify terms,” “prophecy ≠ invulnerability,” “other meanings possible,” “not a valid proof.”
- **Required meaning:** Explain why safety does **not** follow—via ambiguity, source limits, missing clarification, and/or failed inference. Treating the apparitions as straightforward proof, or only summarizing Act 5, fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Explains ambiguity and/or source limits and why safety does not follow
- 1 pt: Says the prophecy was unclear but gives little inference analysis
- 0 pts: Treats the apparitions as straightforward proof, only summarizes events, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require all model reasons; one strong inference-limit explanation can earn full credit.
- Do not require formal syllogism form.
- Do not require later-play spoilers beyond the prompt’s Act 5 discoveries.

## Question 4: Part 2 Q2 – Macbeth tool map (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** A strong map includes: **question** (is Macbeth safe from defeat/harm?); **needed discovery** (what Birnam Wood and “woman born” can mean in actual events; whether the source is trustworthy); **clarified term** (moving wood; woman-born); **inference test** (the words do not prove invulnerability); **judgment** (do not act with certainty or moral permission on ambiguous testimony).
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Ordinary-language maps with the same five jobs. Question may be “Can he trust the apparitions?” Discovery may focus on meanings or source. Judgment may be “don’t treat as guarantee,” “investigate meanings,” “don’t rely on safety.”
- **Required meaning:** Coherent Week 14 tool map with the major elements the prompt lists: question, needed discovery, clarified term, inference test, and judgment. Plot summary without tool-map reasoning fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Gives a coherent Week 14 tool map covering the major elements
- 1 pt: Includes some elements but incomplete
- 0 pts: Plot summary without tool-map reasoning, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require identical labels if the five jobs are clearly present.
- Do not require both prophecies fully worked if one is mapped thoroughly and the overall safety question is handled.
- List or paragraph form both OK.

## Question 5: Part 3 Q1 – Calpurnia and Decius (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Calpurnia offers warnings: frightening signs, reported sights, and her dream or fear that danger threatens Caesar. Decius offers an interpretation that turns the bloody statue image into a flattering sign of Caesar giving life to Rome. Calpurnia presents warning material; Decius reframes its meaning.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Calpurnia: omens, dream of Caesar’s statue spouting blood, stay home, danger. Decius: the dream means Romans will revive by Caesar’s blood/influence; flattering reread; go to the Capitol. Accept “Calpurnia warns; Decius reinterprets.”
- **Required meaning:** Distinguish warning evidence/testimony (Calpurnia) from Decius’s interpretation. Blurring both into “people talked to Caesar” is incomplete.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Distinguishes warning evidence/testimony from Decius’s interpretation
- 1 pt: Mentions both speakers but blurs evidence and interpretation
- 0 pts: Incorrect, missing one side entirely, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require quotation of the dream.
- Do not require deciding who is right on this item.
- Ordinary paraphrase is enough.

## Question 6: Part 3 Q2 – Caesar's distorted judgment (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Caesar’s pride, desire to appear fearless, concern for public honor, and susceptibility to Decius’s flattering interpretation may distort his judgment. He prefers the meaning that makes him seem great and brave rather than carefully weighing danger.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Pride, fear of looking weak/cowardly, honor, reputation, flattery, desire to seem above fear, political image, wanting the crown-related glory. Connection: these push him to accept Decius’s reading and go out.
- **Required meaning:** Name a pressure/desire **and** connect it to distorted judgment about Decius’s interpretation (or the decision to go). Treating Decius’s interpretation as obviously proved, with no pressure analysis, fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Names pressure/desire and connects it to Caesar’s judgment
- 1 pt: Says Caesar is proud but gives little connection to interpretation/decision
- 0 pts: Treats Decius’s interpretation as obviously proven, no pressure, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require a fixed list of motives; one well-connected pressure is enough.
- Do not require modern psychology terms.
- Do not require condemning Caesar beyond the judgment-distortion task.

## Question 7: Part 4 Q1 – Comparing tragic failures (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Macbeth acts on ambiguous testimony before discovering its possible meanings and before judging the source rightly. Caesar acts after receiving warnings but accepts a flattering interpretation without enough careful judgment. Both act before discovery and judgment are complete: Macbeth over-infers safety from prophecy; Caesar lets pride and interpretation overpower caution.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Any comparison that uses discovery/proof/judgment (or clear equivalents) for **both** characters: incomplete clarification, acting on unsafe inference, delayed discovery, pride distorting judgment. Students may emphasize different tools if both figures appear.
- **Required meaning:** Compare both characters on acting before discovery/judgment are complete. One-character-only answers are partial. Plot summary only fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Compares both characters using discovery/proof/judgment terms (or clear equivalents)
- 1 pt: Discusses one character well or gives a thin comparison
- 0 pts: Plot summary only, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require equal paragraph length for both if both are present and the incomplete-tool point is clear.
- Do not require identical model contrasts.
- Formal Week 14 labels preferred but ordinary equivalents OK.

## Question 8: Part 4 Q2 – Alternative careful judgment (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** For Macbeth: the apparitions’ words are ambiguous and morally dangerous, so they should not be treated as proof of safety or permission to act. For Caesar: the warnings and dream are uncertain, but Decius’s flattering interpretation is also not proof; Caesar should investigate, delay, or weigh the danger without pride.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Any careful alternative for **one** chosen character that is grounded in the passage and uses Week 14 tools (clarify terms, test inference, seek discovery, resist flattery, suspend certainty). Examples: “Macbeth should not take ‘safe’ as proved”; “Caesar should not accept Decius without checking the danger.”
- **Required meaning:** One careful alternative judgment the character should have made **before acting**, grounded in the chosen passage. Unsupported moralizing (“be nicer”) or pure plot summary fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Gives a plausible careful alternative judgment grounded in the chosen passage with Week 14 reasoning
- 1 pt: Gives advice but with little Week 14 reasoning
- 0 pts: Unsupported moralizing, plot summary only, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Students choose Macbeth **or** Caesar; do not require both.
- Do not require a full alternate plot ending; the judgment itself is the task.
- Do not require formal map labels if the careful judgment clearly uses discovery/clarification/inference/judgment ideas.

**Overall Notes for Grading This Worksheet:**
- Whole points only. Total: 8 × 2 = 16 points.
- Credit students who distinguish evidence from interpretation and who show that late discovery exposes earlier failures of judgment.
- Do not require exact model wording; preserve tool distinctions and text grounding.
