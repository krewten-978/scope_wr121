# U1L10SP — Must Caesar Die?

- Placement: after Week 10
- Product: second handwritten short paper
- Format: 1–2 handwritten pages on separate lined paper
- Score: 10 points
- Sources: *Julius Caesar*, course handouts, and the student's own class notes only
- Student assignment: `assignment.tex` / `assignment.pdf`
- Textual-evidence supplement: `text-supplement.tex` / `text-supplement.pdf`

The paper asks whether Brutus proves that Caesar must die or only that Caesar might become dangerous. It extends Week 10's point-at-issue work into sustained prose.

The scaffold is lighter than `U1L6SP`: students receive one compact planning page rather than a step-by-step three-page sequence. They identify the point at issue, reconstruct the possibility-to-necessity move, weigh counterpressure, and size the conclusion.

The neutral supplement supplies *Julius Caesar* 2.1 (Brutus in the orchard) and 3.2 (Antony's evidence about Caesar), with factual context and vocabulary support. The excerpts are anchors rather than limits; other accurate events may be paraphrased, but quotations may not be invented from memory.

This is not a Krewone answer-box worksheet and has no `template.json` or AI-grading key.
