## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Songbirds form test (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
**Yes; formally valid.** If both premises were true, the conclusion would have to follow. The form is the familiar all-M-are-P, all-S-are-M, therefore all-S-are-P pattern.

Accept “yes,” “valid,” “passes the form test,” or “the conclusion necessarily follows if the premises are granted.”

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Clearly gives a yes/valid judgment on the form test.
- 0 pts: Says invalid/no, discusses only whether the bird claims are factually true, or gives no form judgment.

**Notes for grading:** The prompt does not ask for an explanation, mood, or soundness judgment. Do not require “AAA” or the exact phrase “formally valid.” A factual objection to the premises does not replace the requested form-test answer.

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – ICU nurses form test (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
**No; formally invalid.** From “some nurses do not work night shifts” and “all ICU nurses are nurses,” it does not follow that any of the nurses who do not work nights are ICU nurses. The particular nurses in the first premise could all work outside the ICU.

Accept “no,” “invalid,” “fails the form test,” or an accurate counterexample. This is an O-A-O mood as printed, but that letter pattern does not make the inference valid.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Clearly gives a no/invalid judgment on the form test.
- 0 pts: Says valid/yes, reasons that ICU nurses must inherit a fact asserted only of some unspecified nurses, or gives no form judgment.

**Notes for grading:** The former key’s “valid O-A-O style structure” was a content error. Do not import an unstated premise that some non-night-shift nurses are ICU nurses. The prompt asks for the form test only, so do not require an explanation or the O-A-O label for full credit.

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – Songbirds fact test (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
Either printed premise may be identified, because both are false or at least seriously doubtful:
- **First/major premise:** “All songbirds can fly long distances over open water” is false; being a songbird does not guarantee long-distance open-water flight, and many songbirds do not have that ability or behavior.
- **Second/minor premise:** “All albatrosses are songbirds” is false; albatrosses are seabirds, not members of the songbird group.

The student needs to identify one premise and give a relevant factual reason. The form’s validity does not make either premise true.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Identifies either the first/major or second/minor premise as false or doubtful and gives a relevant reason explaining the factual problem.
- 1 pt: Correctly identifies a doubtful premise but gives no reason, or gives a relevant factual objection without making clear which premise it challenges.
- 0 pts: Claims both premises must be true because the form is valid, objects only to the conclusion, or gives no relevant fact-test answer.

**Notes for grading:** Do not require the first/major premise specifically. The worksheet asks “which premise,” not “which major premise,” and the second premise is also factually false. Accept “not all songbirds cross open water” and “albatrosses are seabirds rather than songbirds” as concise reasons; no taxonomic rank or example species is required.

## Question 4: Part 3 Q1 – Gemstone warranty (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
The form is valid, but the argument proves the conclusion only if its premises pass the fact test. A careful buyer may reasonably doubt or seek evidence for either printed premise—for example, the catalog’s universal warranty statement may have exclusions, fine print, time limits, seller-specific conditions, or inaccurate advertising; alternatively, the claim that Mara’s particular ring qualifies as a gemstone sold in that catalog may need purchase records or product identification. If a required premise is false or unverified, the valid form does not establish coverage.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Distinguishes valid form from factual soundness and identifies at least one concrete premise-level doubt relevant to warranty coverage.
- 1 pt: Says the premises/facts might be false or unverified but gives no relevant detail, or raises a relevant warranty doubt without clearly connecting it to the fact test.
- 0 pts: Calls the form invalid without a factual issue, assumes validity guarantees warranty coverage, or gives an irrelevant consumer complaint.

**Notes for grading:** Do not require all model doubts, a specific type of fine print, or the formal words “sound/unsound.” One plausible challenge to the truth or support of a printed premise is enough. Do not accept mere dislike of the warranty or seller; the doubt must bear on whether all catalog gemstones are covered or whether Mara’s ring belongs to that covered catalog class.

## Question 5: Part 4 Q1 – Lee deadline (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
The categorical form is valid, but the **major premise is false, doubtful, or unsupported**: submitting before a deadline does not by itself show that every such person cares about the process. People may submit early for many other reasons, such as fear of penalties, convenience, reminders, outside pressure, or a desire to finish. Therefore the argument does not prove Lee cares, even though Lee may in fact care.

Accept “valid but unsound,” “the conclusion may be true but the first premise is not established,” or an ordinary-language explanation that deadline compliance is insufficient evidence of caring.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Explains that the universal link from early submission to caring is false/doubtful or unsupported and distinguishes that weak premise from the possible truth of the conclusion.
- 1 pt: Says meeting a deadline does not prove caring but does not identify the universal premise or explain the gap, or merely labels the argument unsound without a reason.
- 0 pts: Says the conclusion follows as a proved fact merely because Lee submitted early, rejects the argument only because Lee might not care without addressing the premise, or gives an unrelated objection.

**Notes for grading:** Do not require a particular alternative motive or the technical word “unsound.” Do not call this form invalid: if both premises were true, the conclusion would follow. The defect is the unsupported factual generalization, not the AAA structure.

## Question 6: Part 5 Q1 – Review: maples oaks (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
Canonical answer: **formally invalid because of an undistributed middle**. “Trees” is shared by the premises, but knowing that maples and oaks are both kinds of trees does not establish that oaks are maples.

Accept ordinary-language explanations such as “two groups can belong to the same larger class without being the same,” “trees does not connect oaks to maples,” or a counterexample in which oaks and maples are distinct tree classes.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Says invalid and accurately explains the shared-broad-class/middle-term failure or provides a clear counterexample.
- 1 pt: Correctly says invalid but provides no reason or only the unexplained phrase “undistributed middle.”
- 0 pts: Says valid, claims all trees are maples, or gives no judgment.

**Notes for grading:** Do not require the technical phrase “undistributed middle.” If the phrase is used, it must refer to “trees” failing to connect oaks and maples to earn explanation credit.
