# U1L6BLE – Lit Example Worksheet Answer Key & Rubric

**Total Points:** 16

**Paired reader:** `units/elements-of-logic-week6b/lit-examples/U1L6BLE_lit_example_reader.tex` / `.pdf`.

**Paired worksheet:** `graded-assignments/U1L6BLE/worksheet.tex` / `.pdf` (Assignment ID **U1L6BLE**).

**Purpose:** Students apply Week 6B compound tools—either/or, false choice, if/then, antecedent/consequent, conditional traps, form vs fact—to Brutus’s funeral either/or and Macbeth prophecy/conditional pressure.

## Grading Vocabulary for This Key

- **Either/or:** alternatives offered as a forced choice.
- **False choice:** an either/or that ignores other live options.
- **If/then / conditional:** a link from condition (antecedent) to result (consequent).
- **Affirming the consequent:** treating a later result as proof of the earlier condition/chain.
- **Form test:** does the conclusion follow if compound premises are granted?
- **Fact test:** are key supporting claims true, fixed, and proved?
- **Unproved remainder:** what a careful listener still needs before accepting the forced conclusion.

Plot summary alone does not earn points. Grade for compound diagnosis.

## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Brutus either/or branches (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
Brutus offers a choice like: **either** Caesar lives and the people die as slaves, **or** Caesar is dead and the people live as free men.

Accept close paraphrases that keep both branches: Caesar living + slavery/loss of freedom versus Caesar dead + freedom.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: States both branches clearly as an either/or
- 1 pt: Captures only one branch, or states a related conclusion without the two-sided choice
- 0 pts: Mere plot summary (“Brutus speaks after the murder”) with no either/or map

**Notes for grading:** Exact Shakespeare wording is not required. Do not require the label “disjunction.”

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – Omitted live option (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
Any concrete omitted option a careful Roman might still want, for example:
- restrain Caesar’s power without killing him;
- exile, legal check, or temporary limit on power;
- wait for clearer proof of tyranny/ambition before blood;
- reform through Senate process;
- accept some risk under Caesar while rejecting slavery language as overstated.

**Why it matters:** If another live path exists, the either/or cannot by itself force the killing as the only route to freedom.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Names at least one concrete omitted live option and explains why the forced choice is incomplete
- 1 pt: Names an omitted option with little explanation, or explains false-choice pressure without a concrete option
- 0 pts: No omitted option; only restates Brutus’s two branches

**Notes for grading:** Do not require a modern political program. One historically plausible alternative is enough.

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – Terms to fix (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
Any of these (or a tightly related pair) with a brief reason:
- **slave / free**
- **ambitious**
- **loved Rome / loved Caesar** as moral trump language

The term must be fixed because the either/or only forces a verdict if those words mean what Brutus needs them to mean for all hearers.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Identifies a passage-grounded contested term/pair and explains why fixing it matters to the choice
- 1 pt: Names a relevant term without explaining the stakes, or gives a thin stakes note
- 0 pts: No term work; only retells the speech

**Notes for grading:** Accept one strong term. Do not require all three.

## Question 4: Part 2 Q2 – Fact claim still needing proof (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
The ambition/tyranny claim (or that Caesar living entails slavery for Rome) still needs proof. Even a cleaned either/or does not justify the killing unless the dangerous-ambition premise is established with evidence a careful Roman could test.

Accept: “Caesar was ambitious,” “Caesar would make them slaves,” “killing was necessary for freedom,” or “no lesser restraint would work,” provided the student marks it as still needing proof.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Identifies a concrete unproved factual/moral premise tied to justifying the killing or the slavery claim
- 1 pt: Says “needs more proof” without specifying what
- 0 pts: Treats Brutus’s speech as already fully proved, or gives only emotional reaction

**Notes for grading:** Do not require a full evidence list. Naming the ambition/slavery necessity claim is enough.

## Question 5: Part 3 Q1 – Macbeth if/then reconstruction (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
Any faithful reconstruction with labels, for example:
- **Antecedent:** the sisters speak truly / the prophecy holds.
- **Consequent:** Macbeth shall be king / the golden round is ahead.
- Or: **If** Cawdor has come true, **then** the greater promise is nearer.
- Or: **If** the crown is promised, **then** hesitation/impediments should be removed.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Clear if/then with identifiable antecedent and consequent grounded in Passage 2
- 1 pt: Conditional idea present but labels missing or one side vague
- 0 pts: No conditional reconstruction; pure plot summary

**Notes for grading:** Accept “if-part/then-part.” Do not require a quotation.

## Question 6: Part 3 Q2 – Cawdor does not prove kingship chain (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
Cawdor’s fulfillment may support hope or increase credibility, but it does **not** by itself prove the whole kingship destiny. Treating the fulfilled smaller title as proof of the larger promise is (or is like) **affirming the consequent** / using a result to prove a broader condition-chain. Other explanations remain: chance, political appointment, manipulation, partial truth, or a prophecy that is dangerous rather than complete.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Explains that a fulfilled part does not establish the whole chain / larger promise, with a compound-logic reason
- 1 pt: Says it doesn’t fully prove kingship but gives no reason, or only “prophecies are fake”
- 0 pts: Says Cawdor proves Macbeth must become king

**Notes for grading:** Exact phrase “affirming the consequent” is not required. “Later success doesn’t prove the whole prediction” is enough if clearly applied.

## Question 7: Part 4 Q1 – Compare forms and remainders (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Caesar passage:** main compound form = **either/or**; remains unproved = ambition/slavery necessity, fairness of terms, or exhaustiveness of options (any one).
- **Macbeth passage:** main compound form = **if/then** (conditional prophecy/ambition pressure); remains unproved = that the sisters’ full promise is true, that Cawdor proves kingship, or that murderous means are justified (any one).

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Correctly assigns either/or to Caesar and if/then to Macbeth (or equivalent) and states one unproved remainder for each
- 1 pt: Forms mostly right but remainders missing/thin, or remainders good but one form mislabeled
- 0 pts: No comparative compound diagnosis

**Notes for grading:** If a student notes both forms appear in both plays but still identifies the dominant pressure in each passage as required, accept.

## Question 8: Part 4 Q2 – Vivid compound ≠ sound (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
A short paragraph explaining that either/or or if/then language can feel necessary because it is vivid, moral, prophetic, or urgent, while still failing form or fact tests. Must include at least one concrete detail from Caesar or Macbeth (slave/free framing; ambition assertion; Cawdor-to-king leap; golden round pressure).

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Clear persuasion-vs-proof point + one concrete textual detail + compound angle
- 1 pt: General persuasion-vs-proof comment with weak/no detail, or detail without the compound point
- 0 pts: Plot summary only, or no relevant paragraph

**Notes for grading:** Do not require both plays in Q2. One well-used detail is enough.
