# U1L14LL – Logic Lab Answer Key & Rubric

**Total Points:** 16

**Paired worksheet:** `graded-assignments/U1L14LL/worksheet.tex` / `.pdf` (Assignment ID **U1L14LL**).

**Paired reading:** Week 14 — reasoning, discovery, proof, observation, testimony, memory, imagination, judgment, and the intellectual-tool map.

## Grading Vocabulary for This Key

- **Discovery:** finding or learning a fact not yet known.
- **Proof:** showing that a conclusion follows from given reasons or evidence.
- **Observation:** direct attention to facts, events, measurements, records, or evidence.
- **Testimony:** information received from another source or witness.
- **Imagination / hypothesis:** a possible explanation proposed for testing; useful for inquiry but not proof by itself.
- **Memory:** retained knowledge that can suggest a pattern but may need confirmation.
- **Judgment:** deciding which logic tool is needed and how strongly the evidence supports the conclusion.
- **Intellectual-tool map:** question or problem; needed discovery; clarified terms or claims; inference/proof test; judgment about relevance, probability, or application.
- **Review terms:** prior-week source test (authority, relevant evidence) and induction test (examples, overclaim, revised claim).

Week 14 rewards tool choice: students should not force every problem into one method. Credit clear distinctions among discovery, proof, clarification, inference testing, and judgment.

## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Gym roof leak (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** The leak must still be **discovered** by observation, records, inspection, maintenance reports, or testimony from people who saw water. Reasoning can then organize the evidence and test a conclusion such as whether this roof leaks, where it leaks, or whether age is a likely cause. A general argument that old roofs often leak does **not** by itself prove this roof leaks.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Discovery: “look at/inspect the gym roof,” “check for water stains,” “maintenance records,” “ask custodians.” After discovery: “then argue from the evidence,” “test whether age caused it,” “infer from observed water.” Reject “perfect general argument = this roof leaks.”
- **Required meaning:** Separate needed fact-discovery about **this** roof from what reasoning can do **after** facts are in. Treating the general old-roofs argument as proof of the fact fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Separates needed discovery from later reasoning/proof
- 1 pt: Says “check the roof” but gives little distinction from the general argument
- 0 pts: Treats the general argument as proof of the fact, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require a fixed list of inspection methods; one clear discovery path is enough.
- Do not require formal words “discovery” and “proof” if the split is clear.
- Do not require proving the roof does or does not leak.

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – Deadline pattern (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** **Discovery:** the team noticed a repeated pattern among missed deadlines this month (each involved unclear instructions). **Inference:** unclear instructions may be part of the problem. **Claim size:** careful because it says “may be part,” not “always causes” or “is the only cause.”
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Discovery: “noticed the pattern / every missed deadline had unclear instructions.” Inference: “unclear instructions contribute / may cause delays.” Careful size: “hedged,” “not universal,” “not the only cause,” “says may/part.”
- **Required meaning:** All three: discovery (pattern), inference (instructions role), and whether the conclusion is sized carefully (yes—hedged). Treating the pattern as universal proof fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Names discovery, inference, and careful claim size
- 1 pt: Identifies the pattern but not the claim size, or two of three elements
- 0 pts: Treats the pattern as a universal proof, misses the inference entirely, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the word “induction.”
- Do not require students to invent more causes; recognizing the careful “may be part” sizing is enough.
- Ordinary language for all three elements is fine.

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – Fairness dispute (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** The first needed tool-work is **term clarification** because “fair” is used in two different senses: same rule for everyone versus treatment adjusted to different needs. Until the meaning is clarified, the sides may be partly in a verbal dispute and cannot judge the real policy disagreement clearly.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “define fair,” “clarify the term,” “sort out two meanings,” “verbal dispute / mixed dispute first,” “agree what fair means before arguing policy.” Why: two senses (equal rule vs different needs), talking past each other, can’t judge the real disagreement yet.
- **Required meaning:** Identify clarification of “fair” (or resolving two senses) as first tool-work and explain why. Immediately choosing a policy side without clarification fails full credit.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Identifies term clarification / two-senses work and explains why
- 1 pt: Says define “fair” without explaining the two senses or why first
- 0 pts: Immediately chooses a side without clarification, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the formal label “verbal dispute” if two senses + clarify first is clear.
- Do not require settling which sense of fair is correct.
- Do not require a full Week 15 dispute classification unless the student uses it accurately.

## Question 4: Part 2 Q2 – Fundraiser expenses (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** The group must **discover expenses** as well as revenue (records, receipts, net profit). After that, the **proof question** is whether the fundraiser truly succeeded by the relevant standard: more gross money, more net money, better participation, or another clarified goal.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Discover: expenses, costs, net profit, whether costs rose with revenue. Remaining proof/judgment: “did it succeed by net?,” “what does success mean?,” “is more revenue enough?”
- **Required meaning:** Both needed financial discovery (expenses/net) and a remaining proof or success-standard question. Accepting higher revenue alone as settled success fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Names needed financial discovery and the remaining proof/standard question
- 1 pt: Mentions expenses only, or only the success-standard question
- 0 pts: Accepts higher revenue alone as settled success, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require a full accounting method.
- Do not require one exclusive definition of success; noting that the standard must be clear is enough.
- Ordinary language is fine.

## Question 5: Part 3 Q1 – Plant hypothesis (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** The light explanation is a useful **hypothesis** (imagination proposing a testable idea) but not proof until tested. Next: observation/method—compare similar plants in window and non-window locations, control watering/plant type, measure growth, check whether light rather than another factor explains the difference.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Hypothesis not proof: “guess to test,” “possible explanation,” “not yet shown.” Next method: controlled comparison, measure growth, change only light, multiple plants, record results. Accept other sound experimental ideas.
- **Required meaning:** Distinguish hypothesis from proof and name a real observation/method next. Treating the imagined explanation as proven fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Distinguishes hypothesis from proof and names a real observation/method
- 1 pt: Says “test it” vaguely without a concrete method, or only one side clear
- 0 pts: Treats the imagined explanation as proven, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require formal experimental-design vocabulary.
- Do not require proving light is or is not the cause.
- One concrete next test is enough.

## Question 6: Part 3 Q2 – Library memory (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Memory supplies a relevant prior case or starting expectation, but it does **not** settle this year’s situation. Stronger judgment would come from current observation, library schedule, librarian testimony, exam-week policy, room-reservation records, or recent experience from this year.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Memory role: “starting point,” “past pattern,” “suggests it might be quiet,” “not proof for this year.” Strengthen: check this year’s hours/policy, ask librarian, visit, see current noise, official exam-week rules.
- **Required meaning:** Explain memory’s limited role and give relevant confirming evidence (testimony or observation). Treating last year’s memory as certainty fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Explains memory’s role and gives relevant confirming evidence
- 1 pt: Mentions checking this year but not memory’s limited role, or only one side
- 0 pts: Treats last year’s memory as certainty, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require both testimony and observation; one strong confirmer is enough with the memory limit.
- Do not require the word “memory” if “what I remember from last year” is clearly limited.
- Do not require predicting the library will be loud or quiet.

## Question 7: Part 4 Q1 – Bike-lane tool map (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer (map elements):**
  - **Question:** Did the bike lane make streets safer, and/or should it stay?
  - **Needed discovery:** Compare police crash reports, hospital injury records, traffic-speed data, and definitions/counting methods before and after the lane; do not rely only on group-reported crashes or two driver trips.
  - **Clarification:** What “safer,” “crashes dropped,” and “traffic is worse” mean; whether success is fewer crashes, fewer injuries, lower speeds, or acceptable tradeoffs; “safer streets matter most” does not settle the empirical question alone.
  - **Inference test:** Whether partial reports (cycling group’s limited count; two longer trips) support broad conclusions.
  - **Judgment:** Weigh broader evidence, avoid one-sided samples, decide whether safety evidence supports keeping, changing, or further studying the lane.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Ordinary-language maps hitting the same jobs. Careful judgment may be “need the city data first,” “neither side proved,” or “don’t decide keep/remove yet.”
- **Required meaning:** Prompt asks for question, what must be discovered, what terms/claims need clarification, what inference needs testing, and what judgment is needed. Accepting one side without Week 14 tool analysis fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Includes question, needed discovery, clarification, inference test, and judgment (ordinary labels OK)
- 1 pt: Includes some map elements but incomplete
- 0 pts: Accepts one side without Week 14 analysis, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require every data source named if the student clearly requires broader before/after evidence beyond advocacy samples.
- Do not require formal labels if the five jobs are present.
- Do not require a final keep/remove verdict; “study further” is a valid judgment.
- Quotation optional.

## Question 8: Part 5 Q1 – Review: athlete app authority (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** The argument uses celebrity or endorsement authority from a famous athlete. More relevant evidence would include independent reviews, learning data, teacher/student results, privacy/security checks if relevant, or expertise from educators or study-design researchers.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Authority used: “fame,” “celebrity endorsement,” “athlete says so,” “irrelevant authority.” Better evidence: studies, user learning outcomes, expert education reviews, independent tests.
- **Required meaning:** Identify the weak/celebrity authority and name more relevant evidence. Treating fame as enough proof fails.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Identifies weak/celebrity authority and more relevant evidence
- 0 pts: Treats fame as enough proof, only one side, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the exact phrase “appeal to authority.”
- One clear better-evidence type is enough.
- Review skill is Week 13-style source test, not Week 14 discovery map.

## Question 9: Part 5 Q2 – Review: flashcards overclaim (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** **Examples:** four students improved after using flashcards. **Overclaim:** flashcards guarantee higher grades for every student. **Revised claim:** flashcards may help some students improve, or they are worth trying/testing with more students and subjects.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Examples: “four students improved.” Overclaim: “guarantees higher grades for every student,” “works for all.” Revised: “may help some,” “helped these four,” “worth testing.”
- **Required meaning:** All three: examples, overclaim, narrowed revised claim.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Names examples, overclaim, and narrowed revised claim
- 0 pts: Missing a required element, keeps the guarantee claim, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the formal word “overclaim” if “too strong for everyone” is clear.
- Do not require exact model revision wording.
- Review skill is Week 12 induction sizing.
