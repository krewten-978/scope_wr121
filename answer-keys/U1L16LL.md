# U1L16LL – Logic Lab Answer Key & Rubric

**Total Points:** 16

**Paired worksheet:** `graded-assignments/U1L16LL/worksheet.tex` / `.pdf` (Assignment ID **U1L16LL**).

**Paired reading:** Week 16 — persuasion vs proof, logos/ethos/pathos, audience, rhetorical force, and rhetoric-vs-proof analysis.

## Grading Vocabulary for This Key

- **Persuasion:** what makes an audience believe, feel, or act.
- **Proof:** reasons and evidence that actually support a claim.
- **Logos / ethos / pathos:** reason-structure-evidence; speaker character/credibility; emotion.
- **Rhetorical force:** how powerfully a message lands, whether or not it proves.
- **Claim under proof:** the exact conclusion that still needs support.
- **Rhetoric-vs-proof map:** claim; audience/desired response; logos; ethos; pathos; proved; only suggested; force matches/exceeds/replaces proof.
- **Review terms:** Week 15 verbal/real/mixed dispute map; Week 10 missing-the-point / proof-to-claim match.

Week 16 rewards respecting rhetoric’s power without equating applause with proof. When the prompt asks students to label logos/ethos/pathos, those three jobs are required; ordinary descriptions of the moves can still earn credit if the three roles are clear. Grade map **jobs**, not exclusive model wording.

## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Blue out announcement (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Desired action/claim-pressure: wear blue, fill the gym, show up as champions. Audience: the student body. Main rhetorical force: pathos / identity pride and belonging (“if you care,” “champions”), not a proof that attendance causes championship success.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Action: wear blue, fill the gym, attend, support the team. Audience: students, school, peers. Force: pathos, identity, belonging, pride, peer pressure, loyalty language. Accept “ethos of school pride” only if clearly about identity pressure rather than speaker credibility; prefer pathos/identity. Do not require denying every logos scrap if the **main** force is identity/pathos.
- **Required meaning:** All three prompt elements: main claim/desired action, audience, and main rhetorical force. Restating the announcement without analysis fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Names action/claim, audience, and rhetorical force accurately
- 1 pt: Names two of the three elements
- 0 pts: Only restates the announcement, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require formal “pathos” if identity/emotion force is clearly described.
- Do not require proving the announcement fails; naming the force is enough.
- Do not require both wear-blue and fill-gym if the desired action cluster is clear.

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – Weekend work post (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Claim under proof: teachers who assign weekend work do not respect family time / are not “real educators.” Emotion/character pressure: indignation; protective pathos about childhood; ethos attack on teachers who assign weekend work (real vs not-real educators).
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Claim: weekend homework = disrespect for family; such teachers aren’t real educators; assigning weekend work is wrong for family reasons. Pressure: guilt, anger, shame, “protect childhood,” character smear of teachers, loaded “real educators.” One clear pressure move is enough.
- **Required meaning:** Claim under proof **and** one emotion or character pressure. Agreeing/disagreeing with the post without analysis fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: States the claim under proof and one clear pressure move
- 1 pt: Gives claim or pressure only
- 0 pts: Agrees or disagrees without analysis, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require both pathos and ethos if one pressure move is clear (prompt asks for one).
- Do not require formal “claim under proof” wording.
- Do not require students to refute the post.

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – Drain bond speech (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Ethos: coached youth soccer for twelve years / local standing. Pathos: photos of flooded basements; child sleeping upstairs. Logos: vote for the drain bond as a means to reduce flooding/harm (means-end reason). Strong answers label all three without treating photos alone as full engineering proof.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Ethos: local coach, trusted community member, long service. Pathos: flooded basements, kids upstairs, fear/sympathy photos. Logos: bond → less flooding / protect children as policy reason; vote as solution to described harm. Ordinary descriptions acceptable if the three roles are distinguishable.
- **Required meaning:** Prompt asks for one logos, one ethos, and one pathos move — **all three**. Emotional-only or persuasive-only answers fail full credit.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Labels logos, ethos, and pathos accurately
- 1 pt: Labels two appeals clearly
- 0 pts: Only says the speech is emotional or persuasive, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Accept ordinary-language role descriptions if each of the three moves is correctly placed.
- Do not require formal Greek terms if “credibility / emotion / reason” are clearly matched to the right lines.
- Do not require evaluating whether the bond is good policy.
- Photos can be pathos (and weak suggestive evidence); do not demand engineering data for logos if a means-end vote reason is identified.

## Question 4: Part 2 Q2 – Club recruitment (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Strongest appeal often ethos/association or weak inductive logos (past presidents’ college results). Still needed before “join us if you are serious about your future” follows: whether the club caused those outcomes; whether the pattern will continue; whether coding/club work fits this freshman; what “serious about your future” means.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Strongest: ethos (success association), logos (inductive track record), weak cause claim, peer prestige. Still needs proof: causation vs correlation, sample size, fit for this student, definition of “serious,” other paths to college, selection effects. One clear strongest-appeal judgment + one clear missing proof burden is enough.
- **Required meaning:** Strongest appeal **and** what still needs proof. Accepting the pitch as proof fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Names strongest appeal and what still needs proof
- 1 pt: One of those elements only
- 0 pts: Accepts the pitch as proof, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the model’s full missing-proof checklist; one well-aimed gap is enough.
- Do not require one official “strongest” label if the chosen appeal is defensible and the proof gap is real.
- Ordinary language is fine.

## Question 5: Part 3 Q1 – Halftime speech (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Rhetorically, the speech may increase energy, aggression, confidence, or team unity. It has **not** proved that the other team is soft and scared, nor that louder play will win. Force can exceed proof.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Rhetorical effect: motivation, hype, louder play, unity, confidence, intimidation theater. Unproved: opponent is soft/scared; that the claim is true; that the speech guarantees a win. Accept clear force-vs-proof wording such as “force exceeds proof,” “hype without evidence,” or “motivates but does not show the other team is soft.”
- **Required meaning:** Separate possible rhetorical effect from the unproved claim about the other team. Treating the speech as proof the other team is soft fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Separates possible rhetorical effect from unproved claim
- 1 pt: Mentions energy or unproved insult only
- 0 pts: Treats the speech as proof the other team is soft, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require both “soft” and “scared” to be listed as unproved if the opponent-claim is clearly unproved.
- Do not require formal “rhetorical force” wording.
- Do not require sports strategy beyond the force/proof split.

## Question 6: Part 3 Q2 – Coding club flyer (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Supported smaller claim (if the data are real): the coding club was associated with higher average math scores last semester. Overreaching conclusion: every student should drop music for coding. The leap ignores other causes, student differences, the value of music, and whether correlation equals exclusive priority.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Smaller claim: scores rose with the club; association last semester; limited correlation claim. Overclaim: everyone must drop music; coding for all; music is worthless; universal policy leap. Why overreach: correlation ≠ causation, other factors, not every student, music has value, sample limits.
- **Required meaning:** Separate limited support from the universal “drop music” overclaim. Accepting the “therefore every student” leap fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Separates limited support from overclaim
- 1 pt: Notices overclaim with thin explanation
- 0 pts: Accepts the “therefore every student” leap, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the model’s full list of ignored factors; one clear overclaim diagnosis is enough with the limited claim identified.
- Do not require formal “induction” vocabulary.
- Conditional “if the data are real” is optional sophistication, not required.

## Question 7: Part 4 Q1 – Town meeting map (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Main claim: approve the cooling-center budget tonight. Audience/desired response: town meeting voters; immediate yes vote and moral solidarity. Logos: cracked bottle as heat evidence; consultants vs children contrast (partly suggestive). Ethos: grew up on the block; known family. Pathos: kids without air; paperwork-over-children charge. Proved or supported only weakly/local-sensory: heat hardship is vivid; speaker is local. Only suggested: consultants wasted money; delay equals choosing paperwork over children; tonight’s budget is the right remedy and amount. Force often **exceeds** or **replaces** full proof because applause and moral framing outrun budget evidence.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Ordinary-language map covering the prompt’s jobs: main claim; audience/desired response; logos; ethos; pathos; what is proved; what is only suggested; force matches/exceeds/replaces proof. Specific line assignments may vary slightly if roles stay coherent. Force judgment: exceeds or replaces is strongest; “matches” only if carefully limited to a small claim the student defends.
- **Required meaning:** Prompt lists eight map jobs. Grade those jobs in substance. Scene summary or cheering a side without force-vs-proof analysis fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Completes a coherent map with force-vs-proof judgment
- 1 pt: Includes some map elements but incomplete
- 0 pts: Only summarizes the scene or cheers a side, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the model’s full inventory under proved/suggested; each job present with accurate content is enough.
- Do not require formal Greek terms if reason/credibility/emotion moves are correctly placed.
- Do not require deciding the correct budget policy.
- Compact bullets or paragraph form is fine.

## Question 8: Part 5 Q1 – Review: neighborly fence (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Contested term: **neighborly**. Likely **mixed** (or verbal leading to real): one meaning centers blocking an ugly view; the other centers shared light/sight lines. Clarification helps; a real preference conflict may remain.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Term: neighborly, being a good neighbor. Classification: mixed preferred; verbal; verbal-with-real-underneath. Brief support tying two meanings to the classification.
- **Required meaning:** Contested term **and** a plausible verbal/real/mixed classification. Picking a winner without dispute analysis fails.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Names contested term and a plausible classification
- 0 pts: Picks a winner without dispute analysis, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require predicting the final preference outcome.
- Do not require full seven-part dispute map; prompt asks term + classification.
- One-point item: both elements needed.

## Question 9: Part 5 Q2 – Review: field trip bus (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Claim needing proof: the field trip should be canceled. Clean seats do not answer safety, cost, educational value, timing, or other cancel-worthy reasons. The reason **misses the point**.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Needed claim: cancel the trip. Mismatch: clean seats irrelevant to cancel decision; wrong kind of reason; proof does not meet the claim; missing-the-point / irrelevant proof.
- **Required meaning:** State the claim that needed proof and explain why clean seats fail to meet it. Treating clean seats as sufficient cancel proof fails.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: States needed claim and explains mismatch
- 0 pts: Treats clean seats as sufficient cancel proof, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the formal label “missing the point” if the mismatch is clear.
- Do not require listing every possible cancel reason; explaining irrelevance of clean seats is enough.
- One-point item: claim + mismatch both needed.
