## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Command vs. proposition (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** No. “Please submit the form by Friday” is a command, request, directive, or instruction rather than a proposition.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “not a claim,” “it tells someone what to do,” “it cannot simply be judged true or false,” or equivalent ordinary language.
- **Required meaning:** The response must answer **no** and explain that the sentence directs an action rather than asserting something that is true or false.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Correctly says it is not a proposition and gives the required command/request or truth-value reason
- 0 pts: Says it is a proposition, gives no reason, or confuses politeness/future timing with what makes a proposition

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the formal words “imperative,” “truth value,” or “proposition” in the explanation.
- “No, because it is asking/telling someone to submit a form” is sufficient.
- Do not accept “no because it has not happened yet”; future-tense claims can still be propositions.

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – Volunteers briefing (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical subject:** Volunteers.
- **Canonical predicate:** People who missed the safety briefing / missed the safety briefing.
- **Canonical quality:** Affirmative.
- **Canonical quantity:** Particular.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** For quality, accept “affirmative,” “positive,” or “it asserts that the predicate applies.” For quantity, accept “particular,” “some,” or “at least one,” provided the response clearly indicates one or more volunteers rather than every volunteer.
- **Required meaning:** All four requested components must be explicit. The response must identify the proposition as asserting that at least one member of the volunteer class belongs to the class of people who missed the briefing.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Correctly and explicitly supplies subject, predicate, affirmative quality, and particular/some quantity
- 0 pts: Omits or misidentifies any requested component, reverses subject and predicate, or supplies only an I-form letter that the prompt did not ask for

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not withhold credit solely because the student writes “some” or “at least one” instead of the formal quantity label “particular.”
- Do not require “people who” in the predicate; “missed the safety briefing” is a clear predicate description.
- “Not all” is not automatically equivalent to the stated “some” claim in this classification response; the student must clearly identify particular scope.

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – No smartphones (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical form:** E.
- **Canonical quality:** Negative.
- **Canonical quantity:** Universal.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** For quality, accept “negative,” “denies membership,” or an unambiguous statement that the proposition excludes smartphones from the permitted-device class. For quantity, accept “universal,” “all smartphones are covered by the denial,” “no smartphones,” or “none,” when the response clearly indicates the entire smartphone subject class.
- **Required meaning:** The response must explicitly supply all three requested classifications: E form, negative quality, and universal scope.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Explicitly identifies E, negative quality, and universal quantity using formal terms or semantically clear alternatives
- 0 pts: Omits or misidentifies any one of the three requested classifications, or gives only “E” and relies on the grader to infer quality and quantity

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the exact words “negative” and “universal” if the student separately and unambiguously describes denial and whole-class scope.
- “No” can communicate both denial and universal scope in the proposition, but the response must still make clear that it is answering both requested dimensions rather than merely repeating the sentence.
- Do not accept O/particular-negative language; the proposition excludes every smartphone in the lab from the permitted-device class.

## Question 4: Part 2 Q2 – At least one finalist (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical form:** I.
- **Canonical quality:** Affirmative.
- **Canonical quantity:** Particular.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** For quality, accept “affirmative,” “positive,” or “asserts that a finalist belongs to the three-language-speaker class.” For quantity, accept “particular,” “some,” “one or more,” or “at least one.”
- **Required meaning:** The response must explicitly supply all three requested classifications and preserve the claim that one or more finalists—not necessarily all finalists—speak three languages.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Explicitly identifies I, affirmative quality, and particular/some quantity
- 0 pts: Omits or misidentifies any one of the three classifications, gives only “I,” or strengthens the proposition to universal/all finalists

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not withhold credit because the student uses the quantifier “some” or “at least one” instead of the formal category “particular.”
- “One” is acceptable only when used as “at least one/one or more,” not when the response claims exactly one finalist.
- Do not accept “most” or “all”; those materially change the proposition’s strength.

## Question 5: Part 3 Q1 – Only badge holders (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical standard-form translation:** All people who may enter the server room are employees with a valid badge.
- **Canonical subject:** People who may enter the server room / permitted server-room entrants.
- **Canonical predicate:** Employees with a valid badge / valid-badged employees.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “All authorized server-room entrants are valid-badge-holding employees” or another clear A-form paraphrase. The classes may be named differently if their direction and membership conditions are unchanged.
- **Required meaning:** The translation must preserve the direction of **only**: permission to enter implies being an employee with a valid badge. It must be a universal affirmative standard-form claim, and the chosen subject and predicate must be stated separately or labeled unambiguously.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Gives a correct universal-affirmative translation in the required direction and explicitly identifies its subject and predicate
- 0 pts: Omits the subject or predicate identification, reverses the implication, gives only “all badge holders may enter,” or changes/removes the employee or valid-badge condition

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the model sentence word for word or the letter A, because the prompt asks for the translated proposition and its terms rather than the form label.
- Do not accept “All employees with valid badges may enter.” That converse grants entry to every badge-holding employee and reverses what **only** establishes.
- “Only employees may enter” is incomplete because the source also requires a valid badge.

## Question 6: Part 3 Q2 – Not every ticket holder (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical standard-form translation:** Some ticket holders are not people who were seated before the show began.
- **Canonical subject:** Ticket holders.
- **Canonical predicate:** People seated before the show began / people who were seated before the show began.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “At least one ticket holder was not seated before the show began” or another clear O-form paraphrase, provided the student also identifies the subject and predicate.
- **Required meaning:** The translation must preserve particular quantity and negative quality: one or more ticket holders lacked the predicate. It must not claim that no ticket holders were seated or that most/all were not seated.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Gives a correct particular-negative standard-form translation and explicitly identifies its subject and predicate
- 0 pts: Omits term identification, reverses the terms, changes the claim to universal negative, or merely repeats “not every” without translating it into clear standard form

**Notes for grading:**
- Accept “some,” “at least one,” or “one or more” for particular quantity.
- Do not require the letter O because the prompt asks for a translation, subject, and predicate rather than a form label.
- Do not treat “some” as “some but not all”; in the course reading it means at least one and does not exclude the possibility of many.

## Question 7: Part 4 Q1 – Distribution on Part 3 Q2 (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical subject status:** *Ticket holders* is undistributed because the O proposition makes the claim only about some/at least one ticket holder, not the whole ticket-holder class.
- **Canonical predicate status:** *People seated before the show began* is distributed because the selected ticket holder(s) are denied membership in that predicate class as a whole—not merely denied membership in some part of it.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “not distributed” for “undistributed,” “the subject refers only to one or more ticket holders,” and “the predicate is completely excluded/none of the seated-before-show class applies to those selected ticket holders.”
- **Required meaning:** The response must classify both terms and briefly explain each status using the scope of the O proposition.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Correctly identifies the subject as undistributed and predicate as distributed and gives a brief explanation that addresses partial subject scope and whole-class predicate denial
- 1 pt: Correctly classifies both terms but gives no meaningful explanation; or correctly classifies and explains one term while misclassifying or omitting the other
- 0 pts: Reverses both statuses, gives no correct classification, or explains distribution merely as where a word appears in the sentence

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the phrase “O-form rule” or the model’s exact explanation.
- “Some ticket holders means not all ticket holders are being discussed; ‘are not seated-before-show people’ excludes those selected holders from that entire class” is sufficient.
- Do not accept “the predicate is distributed because the proposition is negative” without any indication that the denial reaches the whole predicate class; the prompt explicitly asks for a brief explanation.

## Question 8: Part 4 Q2 – Coaches exchange (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical ambiguity:** Person A leaves quantity unclear: “Coaches” could mean **all/every coach** (a universal claim) or **some/one or more coaches** (a particular claim).
- **Canonical refutation effect:** If A means all coaches are unfair to substitutes, one genuine counterexample—a coach who treats a substitute fairly—can refute the universal claim. If A means only some coaches are unfair, one fair coach does not refute the particular claim.
- **Required qualification from the exact exchange:** Person B’s example counts as that counterexample only if B is a substitute (or the extra practice was treatment of a substitute) and the extra practice genuinely shows fairness. The paragraph does not state those facts, so B’s reply as written does not conclusively refute either reading without them.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “everyone versus at least one,” “universal versus particular,” or “the sentence could be a generalization or an existence claim.” Accept responses that say B **could** refute the all-claim but cannot refute the some-claim, as well as responses that additionally flag the missing information about B’s status or fairness.
- **Required meaning:** The response must identify both possible quantities and explain why a single counterexample has different force against universal and particular claims.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Identifies all-versus-some ambiguity and correctly explains that one relevant fair-coach counterexample could defeat “all” but not “some”; no deduction for noting that B’s example is under-specified
- 1 pt: Identifies the quantity ambiguity but gives only a vague account of refutation; or correctly discusses counterexamples to all versus some without clearly naming both quantities
- 0 pts: Discusses only whether coaches are fair, treats one fair coach as disproving that any unfair coach exists, or never addresses quantity

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the formal labels “universal” and “particular”; “all coaches” versus “some coaches” is sufficient.
- Do not penalize a student for observing that Person B is not explicitly identified as a substitute and that extra practice does not automatically establish fairness. That is a valid close reading of the supplied exchange.
- Do not require students to decide which quantity Person A intended; the point is that A left it unclear.

## Question 9: Part 5 Q1 – Review: four-step software (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical conclusion:** The app must be secure against all future attacks.
- **Canonical stated reason:** The update fixed every bug reported last month.
- **Canonical hidden assumption:** Fixing all bugs reported during one past month guarantees complete security against every future attack or vulnerability.
- **Canonical follow judgment:** No; past reported-bug fixes do not establish protection against all unknown future attacks.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “the app will never be successfully attacked” for the conclusion, “all of last month’s known/reported bugs were fixed” for the reason, assumptions such as “past fixes ensure future safety,” and “not necessarily/insufficient evidence” for the negative judgment.
- **Required meaning:** All four elements of the Week 1 test must be explicit and preserve the gap between a limited past repair record and universal future security.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Correctly supplies all four elements; wording and order may vary if the roles are unambiguous
- 0 pts: Omits any element, swaps conclusion and reason, weakens the conclusion to “the app is safer,” or treats last month’s fixes as proof of all future security

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require a cybersecurity term, fallacy label, or extended explanation.
- “The app is more secure now” is not the stated conclusion; the worksheet makes the much stronger claim “secure against all future attacks.”
- A bare “no” supplies only the follow judgment, not the separately requested conclusion, reason, and assumption.

## Question 10: Part 5 Q2 – Review: right to appeal vs. right answer (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical word:** *right*.
- **Canonical first meaning:** Entitlement, permission, or a protected opportunity to appeal the grade.
- **Canonical second meaning:** Correct, accurate, or true, as applied to the answer.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “allowed to appeal” versus “factually correct,” “a claim/privilege” versus “the correct answer,” or similar wording that clearly separates the two senses.
- **Required meaning:** The response must identify the repeated word and state both contextual meanings.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Identifies *right* and clearly distinguishes entitlement/permission from correctness/truth
- 0 pts: Omits the word or either meaning, treats both uses as the same sense, or gives an unrelated distinction

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the technical terms “entitlement,” “permission,” or “equivocation.”
- “You are allowed to appeal, but that does not make your answer correct” is sufficient.
- Do not accept “right versus wrong” as both meanings unless the first use is also explained as a right/permission to appeal.
