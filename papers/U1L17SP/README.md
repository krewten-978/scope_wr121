# U1L17SP — Who Reasons More Dishonestly with Himself?

- Placement: after Week 17
- Product: late comparative practice paper
- Format: 2–3 handwritten pages on separate lined paper
- Score: 15 points
- Sources: *Macbeth*, *Julius Caesar*, course handouts, and the student's own class notes only
- Student assignment: `assignment.tex` / `assignment.pdf`
- Textual-evidence supplement: `text-supplement.tex` / `text-supplement.pdf`

The paper asks students to compare Macbeth's and Brutus's private reasoning before political murder. Students must define self-deceptive reasoning, reconstruct both arguments, choose fitting tools, give fair counterpressure to both men, and reach a proportional judgment.

The planning page is minimal. Unlike the earlier papers, it does not prescribe a paragraph route: students set a common standard, run two compact autopsies, identify the decisive similarity and difference, and select an independent comparative structure.

The neutral supplement supplies *Macbeth* 1.7 and *Julius Caesar* 2.1, transcribed from the existing `U1L17LE` source set and expanded from repository-verified passages. Analytical reader notes are not copied into the supplement. The excerpts are anchors rather than limits; later events may be paraphrased accurately, but quotations may not be invented from memory.

This is not a Krewone answer-box worksheet and has no `template.json` or AI-grading key.
