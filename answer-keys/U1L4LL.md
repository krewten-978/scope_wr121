## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Fleet inspection premises (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**  
**Premise 1:** All vehicles in the city fleet are inspected every month.  
**Premise 2:** Van 14 is in the city fleet.  
**Conclusion:** Van 14 is inspected every month.

Accept accurate paraphrases: for example, “Every city-fleet vehicle gets a monthly inspection,” “Van 14 belongs to the fleet,” and “Van 14 therefore gets a monthly inspection.” The response must identify both stated supporting claims and the claim they support; sentence order and the labels “Premise 1” and “Premise 2” need not match the model.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Identifies both premises and the conclusion, with each claim preserving the original subject, scope, and monthly-inspection meaning.
- 0 pts: Omits or misclassifies any of the three claims, treats the conclusion as a premise, or materially changes a claim (for example, says only some fleet vehicles are inspected).

**Notes for grading:** Do not require verbatim quotation or the model’s sentence order. A response may abbreviate “vehicles in the city fleet” as “city-fleet vehicles” and “inspected every month” as “monthly inspection.”

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – Fleet terms (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**  
**Minor term:** Van 14 (subject of the conclusion).
**Major term:** inspected every month / things inspected every month (predicate class of the conclusion).
**Middle term:** vehicles in the city fleet / city-fleet vehicles (the class used in both premises but absent from the conclusion).

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Correctly names all three terms. Accept “Van 14,” “inspected every month” or “things/vehicles receiving monthly inspections,” and “city fleet,” “vehicles in the city fleet,” or “city-fleet vehicles,” provided each is assigned to the correct role.
- 0 pts: Any term is missing, assigned to the wrong role, or replaced by a materially different idea.

**Notes for grading:** Do not require the student to add the definitions in parentheses; the prompt asks students to name the terms. Do not accept “vehicles” alone as the middle term if it loses the restricting idea “in the city fleet.”

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – Crocodiles (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**  
**Middle term:** reptiles / the class of reptiles.
**Major premise:** No reptiles are mammals (it contains the conclusion’s predicate term, mammals).
**Minor premise:** All crocodiles are reptiles (it contains the conclusion’s subject term, crocodiles).

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Correctly identifies the middle term and assigns both printed premises to their correct major/minor roles.
- 1 pt: Correctly supplies one or two of those three requested components but makes or omits at least one assignment.
- 0 pts: Supplies none correctly, reverses both premise roles without any correct middle term, or gives unrelated terms/claims.

**Notes for grading:** Accept the exact printed propositions or unambiguous abbreviations such as “the no-reptiles-are-mammals premise” and “the all-crocodiles-are-reptiles premise.” Do not require an explanation of why each premise has its role; the prompt asks only for identification and assignment.

## Question 5: Part 3 Q1 – Medication paragraph (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**  
**Premise 1:** Every medication in the locked cabinet requires a witness when dispensed.  
**Premise 2:** Today’s dose for room 302 came from the locked cabinet.
**Conclusion:** Today’s dose for room 302 required a witness when dispensed.

Accept concise paraphrases that preserve the universal cabinet rule, the source of room 302’s dose, and the witness requirement for that dose. The order of the two premises may vary.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Extracts and correctly classifies both supporting claims and the conclusion of the main argument; irrelevant details about the closet check, interns, and lunch are excluded or clearly treated as distractors.
- 1 pt: Correctly identifies two of the three argument claims, or identifies all three but mislabels one premise/conclusion role.
- 0 pts: Identifies at most one argument claim, builds the answer mainly from distractors, or materially changes the rule or conclusion.

**Notes for grading:** Do not require quotation. Accept “locked-cabinet medication needs a witness” for Premise 1 only if the response retains the every/all scope of the posted rule; a statement merely that witnesses are sometimes used is too weak.

## Question 6: Part 4 Q1 – Tutoring center (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**  
**Middle term:** students who improved last term / people who improved last term.
**Connection judgment:** It does not link the other terms in a way that supports the conclusion. The premises establish that tutoring-center users are among the students who improved and that Jordan improved; they do not establish the converse claim that every student who improved used the tutoring center. Thus Jordan’s improvement does not prove that Jordan used the center.

Accept ordinary-language explanations such as “the argument reverses the first statement,” “improving does not mean Jordan used the center,” or “other causes could explain Jordan’s improvement,” when the response clearly identifies the one-way gap. The technical labels “undistributed middle,” “affirming the consequent,” or “converse error” are not required.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Identifies the middle term and clearly explains that the shared fact of improvement does not license the reverse inference from Jordan improved to Jordan used the tutoring center.
- 1 pt: Correctly identifies the middle term but gives no adequate connection explanation, or correctly explains the reverse/one-way gap but omits or misnames the middle term.
- 0 pts: Says the conclusion follows, offers only “invalid” or “does not connect” without explaining the relevant gap, or provides neither requested component correctly.

**Notes for grading:** Do not require formal fallacy vocabulary. A mere keyword such as “undistributed” earns no explanation credit unless the student connects it to the fact that the premise runs from center use to improvement, not from improvement to center use.

## Question 7: Part 5 Q1 – Review: right / right (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**  
The repeated word is **right**. Its first use means an entitlement, permission, liberty, or legitimate claim to speak; its second use means correct, accurate, or true about the policy.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Identifies “right” and distinguishes both senses: permission/entitlement to speak versus correctness/truth of the policy judgment.
- 0 pts: Omits the repeated word, gives only one sense, or treats both uses as having the same meaning.

**Notes for grading:** Do not require the exact words “entitlement” and “correct.” Accept the listed ordinary-language variants when the first sense concerns authorization or a claim to speak and the second concerns whether the policy view is true or accurate.

## Question 8: Part 5 Q2 – Review: electric scooters (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**  
**Standard form:** E.
**Quality:** negative.
**Quantity:** universal.

For quality, accept “negative,” “denies membership,” or an equally explicit statement that the subject class is excluded from the predicate class. For quantity, accept “universal,” “all electric scooters in this lot,” “no electric scooters in this lot,” or “none of the electric scooters in this lot,” provided the response clearly indicates scope over the entire subject class. The E designation must still be supplied because the prompt explicitly asks for an A/E/I/O form.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Explicitly supplies all three requested components: E form, negative quality, and universal quantity. An answer such as “E, negative, none of the scooters in this lot” earns full credit.
- 0 pts: Omits any requested component; gives A, I, or O; calls the claim affirmative or particular; or uses vague quantity language such as “many,” “most,” or “not everyone.”

**Notes for grading:** Do not withhold credit solely because the student expresses universal quantity with the proposition’s quantifier “no/none” instead of the formal word “universal.” Do not infer the separately requested quality and quantity from “E” alone.
