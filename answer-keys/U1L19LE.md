## Grading Vocabulary for This Key

- **Judgment mirror:** a literary failure used to clarify a reasoning habit, not to claim moral superiority.
- **Warrant:** the principle that explains why textual evidence supports a claim.
- **Counterpressure:** evidence, alternative, or objection that tests a conclusion.
- **Qualification:** a limit that prevents a claim from outrunning the passage.
- **Transfer:** a concrete use of the same habit outside literary analysis.
- **Equivocation / “lies like truth”:** wording that can trap by seeming reassuring while remaining technically fulfillable in an unexpected sense.
- **Possibility-to-necessity:** treating what might happen as enough for what must be done now.

Plot summary alone does not earn points. Quotation is preferred when the prompt asks for words from the passage, but accurate short paraphrase may support full credit when the pressure point is unmistakable. Accept different defensible habits when the textual warrant is explicit.

## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Macbeth observation and responses (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Observation: the messenger reports Birnam Wood appearing to move. First response: Macbeth attacks the witness (“Liar and slave!”). Later response: as the report holds, he begins to doubt the equivocation of the fiend / the promise that seemed to guarantee safety until Birnam Wood moves.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Observation: moving wood; Birnam coming to Dunsinane; messenger’s report contradicting expected security. First response: denial; shooting the messenger; calling the report a lie. Later response: doubts the equivocal promise; sees the words may “lie like truth”; confidence breaks. Ordinary paraphrase OK.
- **Required meaning:** The disconfirming observation **and** a clear contrast between first hostile/denying response and later doubt/revision. Plot summary without the judgment sequence fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Observation and both contrasting responses
- 1 pt: Observation plus one response, or both responses without a clear observation
- 0 pts: Plot summary without judgment sequence, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require exact quotation if both responses and the observation are accurately described.
- Do not require naming “equivocation” if later doubt of the fiend’s promise is clear.
- Do not accept only “Macbeth gets angry” without the later shift.

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – Macbeth claim and warrant (3 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Limited claim: disciplined judgment stays open to disconfirming observation and retests source and terms when facts resist expectation. Habit: source testing, term/equivocation clarification, evidence openness, or intellectual humility under contrary facts. Warrant: willingness to revise confidence when relevant evidence arrives is a mark of disciplined thought; Macbeth’s delayed revision shows the cost of resisting that habit.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Claims may emphasize not attacking the messenger, retesting promises, separating comforting wording from proof, or revising when observation contradicts expectation. Habits and warrants may use ordinary language if the link passage→habit→claim is explicit.
- **Required meaning:** Limited claim about disciplined judgment **and** named habit **and** explicit warrant linking passage to claim. Tool name without connection, or plot/moral verdict only, fails full credit.

**Rubric:**
- 3 pts: Limited claim, accurate habit, and explicit warrant
- 2 pts: Sound claim/habit with only an implied warrant, or warrant clear but claim slightly overbroad
- 1 pt: Tool name without logical connection, or claim with no habit/warrant
- 0 pts: Plot or moral verdict only

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the model’s exact claim sentence.
- Do not require both “source” and “terms” if one accurate habit is warranted by the sequence.
- Do not accept a universal claim (“always test sources or tragedy never happens”) without limit—limits are scored more fully in 2.1, but wild overclaim here caps the score.

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – Macbeth qualification (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** The passage does not prove that source testing prevents every tragedy, that Macbeth becomes fully disciplined, or that correcting one belief repairs prior choices. A valid qualification limits the claim to this moment’s warning about evidence, equivocation, and delayed revision.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Any real passage-sensitive limit: one scene ≠ whole character reform; noticing a trap late ≠ undoing earlier violence; logic tools don’t guarantee good motives; observation can force doubt without producing wisdom. Then a tightened claim scope.
- **Required meaning:** Names what the passage does **not** prove (or a real limit) **and** adds a qualification that keeps the claim from overreaching. Universalizing from the scene fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Real passage-sensitive limit and qualified claim
- 1 pt: General limit without revising scope, or qualified tone without naming what is unproved
- 0 pts: Universalizes from the scene, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the model’s full list of non-proofs; one accurate limit plus a scoped claim is enough.
- Do not require formal “qualification” label if the limited restatement is clear.
- Do not accept “Macbeth is evil so logic doesn’t matter” as a disciplined qualification.

## Question 4: Part 2 Q2 – Macbeth transfer (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Concrete nonliterary setting + pressure + action—e.g. when a workplace report contradicts a favored plan, pause before attacking the messenger, inspect the evidence, and retest the plan’s assumptions.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Settings: work, family, civic life, sports, social media, other classes. Pressures: bad news, contrary data, favored plan threatened, trusted source challenged. Actions: check evidence; retest terms/assumptions; don’t shoot the messenger; revise confidence before acting. Other specific aligned transfers are acceptable.
- **Required meaning:** Concrete setting (from the transfer request) **and** pressure **and** disciplined action. “Do not be like Macbeth” alone fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Setting, pressure, and action are concrete and aligned
- 1 pt: Sensible but generic transfer (missing one concrete element)
- 0 pts: “Do not be like Macbeth” only, pure moralizing, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the workplace example.
- Do not require a formal habit name if the action clearly enacts the warning.
- The prompt foregrounds pressure and action; still require a recognizable nonliterary setting because the prompt says “concrete nonliterary setting.”

## Question 5: Part 3 Q1 – Brutus pressure point (3 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Precise claim: the pressure point is moving from “might change his nature” to “It must be by his death”—treating possible future tyranny as enough for present lethal necessity. Evidence may include the confessed lack of present personal cause and/or the serpent’s-egg analogy.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Claims focused on possibility→necessity, analogy used as proof, preventive killing without present cause, or uncertainty dressed as must. Evidence should use words from the passage or unmistakable short paraphrase of those anchors.
- **Required meaning:** Precise claim about the reasoning pressure point **with** passage evidence and the inferential leap identified. Moral judgment or plot plus a phrase fails full credit.

**Rubric:**
- 3 pts: Precise claim with quoted/accurate evidence and inferential pressure
- 2 pts: Correct claim with thin evidence, or strong evidence with slightly vague pressure point
- 1 pt: Moral judgment or plot plus a phrase
- 0 pts: No reasoning analysis

**Notes for grading:**
- Prefer words from the passage when present; do not withhold full credit solely for accurate close paraphrase if the pressure point is exact.
- Do not require both “no personal cause” and the egg analogy if one solid textual anchor supports the claim.
- Do not require deciding whether Caesar would become a tyrant.

## Question 6: Part 3 Q2 – Brutus warrant and counterpressure (3 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Warrant: disciplined thinkers distinguish possibility from demonstrated necessity, test analogies for relevant similarity, and/or examine less extreme alternatives before lethal conclusions. Fair counterpressure: Rome may face a genuine risk, but the argument still needs evidence of likely tyranny and an account of why restraint or other options cannot answer it.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Warrant principles may emphasize evidence proportion, analogy limits, hidden-assumption tests, or not treating motive/fear as proof. Counterpressure may be: real danger to the republic; Caesar’s ambition signs; limited time; etc.—but must remain a **fair test** the conclusion must answer, not a total dismissal of all concern.
- **Required meaning:** Explicit warrant connecting evidence to one disciplined habit **and** a fair counterpressure/alternative. Tool name or objection without connection fails full credit.

**Rubric:**
- 3 pts: Explicit warrant and fair, relevant counterpressure
- 2 pts: One strong and one partial
- 1 pt: Tool name or objection without connection
- 0 pts: No test of the conclusion

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require multiple warrant principles; one clear habit-linked warrant is enough with a fair counterpressure.
- Do not require the student to refute Brutus completely.
- Do not accept “Brutus is wrong because murder is bad” as a warrant without the logical habit link.

## Question 7: Part 4 Q1 – Compare judgment failures (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Both characters need intellectual humility before a desired or settled conclusion, but the pressures differ. Macbeth protects confidence in an equivocal source until observation breaks it; Brutus turns uncertainty and analogy into preventive necessity before sufficient evidence. Shared missing habits may include testing assumptions, source/term clarification, proportioning conclusions, and attending to counterevidence.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Shared habit phrasing may vary (humility under desire; test before certainty; size claims to evidence; retest sources/analogies). Difference must be meaningful: late forced revision vs early necessity leap; source/equivocation trap vs possibility-to-necessity/analogy; etc.
- **Required meaning:** Shared habit missing (or needed) in both **and** different forms of pressure shown by the two passages. Plot comparison only fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Shared habit plus meaningful difference in pressure
- 1 pt: One side only, generic comparison, or shared habit without differentiated pressure
- 0 pts: Plot comparison only, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Both passages must appear for full credit.
- Do not require equal paragraph length.
- Do not require the model’s exact habit list if one clear shared habit and a real pressure difference are present.

## Question 8: Part 4 Q2 – Miniature portfolio defense (3 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** A coherent closing paragraph using **one** passage that includes all five elements: (1) precise claim about disciplined judgment; (2) passage evidence; (3) explicit warrant; (4) fair qualification; (5) concrete transfer beyond the logic unit. Functions as an argument, not a list of labels.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Either Macbeth or Brutus passage. Compact integrated prose preferred; light labeling OK if the argument still connects. Transfer must leave the logic unit/course for a real setting.
- **Required meaning:** All five elements present and connected. Plot summary only fails. Several elements listed but not connected is partial.

**Rubric:**
- 3 pts: Coherent argument with all five elements
- 2 pts: Sound argument missing one element, or all present but one thin/unconnected
- 1 pt: Several elements listed but not connected
- 0 pts: Plot summary only, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require both passages; the prompt says one passage.
- Do not require equal development of all five elements if each is genuinely present.
- Do not require model wording for claim, warrant, qualification, or transfer.
- Do not accept a transfer that stays inside this unit only.
