## Grading Vocabulary for This Key

- **Disciplined thinker:** one who repeatedly clarifies terms and claims, tests inference, weighs evidence and sources, surfaces assumptions, distinguishes rhetoric from proof, and revises when reasons require it.
- **Controlling claim:** the precise thesis the defense seeks to establish.
- **Portfolio evidence:** a specific artifact and recoverable feature used to support a growth claim.
- **Commentary:** explanation of how an artifact supports the thesis.
- **Warrant:** the principle connecting artifact evidence to the conclusion about growth.
- **Counterpressure:** a fair objection, contrary artifact, or remaining weakness that tests the thesis.
- **Qualification:** a limit that makes the thesis accurate and defensible.
- **Transfer claim:** a concrete action using a logical habit in a setting beyond the course.
- **Review tools:** Week 18 revision-trail standard (original, diagnosis/change, habit, what growth shows); Week 16 rhetoric-vs-proof; Week 17 private argument / public cover / motivated reasoning.

Personalized evidence is required. Grade the quality of the argument and its verifiable artifact references; no single life story, artifact set, or model wording is required. Accept ordinary-language habit names when the logical jobs are clear.

## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Define disciplined thinking (3 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Defines a disciplined thinker through at least three accurate, observable Whately habits (e.g. clarify terms/claims; test inference/form; weigh evidence proportion; test sources; surface assumptions; distinguish rhetoric from proof; revise when reasons require it) and acknowledges one real limit (logic does not supply missing facts, guarantee wisdom or good motives, replace domain expertise, or settle every moral judgment by itself).
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Habit lists may use course names or clear ordinary equivalents that preserve the same jobs. Limits may be phrased as “logic can’t invent facts,” “valid form ≠ true premises,” “proof ≠ persuasion,” “tools don’t guarantee character,” etc. A short coherent definition plus bullets is fine.
- **Required meaning:** Coherent definitional core **and** at least three accurate habits **and** one real limit. “Trying hard / caring / being smart” alone fails. A bare list with no definitional framing is partial.

**Rubric:**
- 3 pts: Coherent definition, three accurate habits, and a real limit
- 2 pts: Strong answer missing one requirement (definition, third habit, or limit), or habits slightly thin
- 1 pt: General care/effort language with little course precision
- 0 pts: Circular, inaccurate, or no relevant definition

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require more than three habits if three accurate ones are present.
- Do not require the model’s exact habit list or limit wording.
- Do not require formal labels if the observable habits are clear.
- Do not accept “logic proves everything” or “logic is just opinions” as accurate limits/habits.

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – Controlling claim (3 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** A specific, supportable pattern of growth the portfolio can prove—e.g. increasingly proportionate claims, more stable terms, more explicit assumptions, better source testing, stronger force-vs-proof distinction, or more reliable revision under pressure. Avoids perfection and mere completion.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Any precise growth thesis tied to reasoning habits rather than grades, neatness, or finishing work. Comparative language (“more…,” “increasingly…,” “better at…”) is welcome but not required if the pattern is specific and testable.
- **Required meaning:** One controlling claim that is specific, arguable, and portfolio-testable as a growth pattern. “I completed the portfolio,” “I got better grades,” or “I am now perfect” fails.

**Rubric:**
- 3 pts: Precise, arguable, portfolio-testable growth thesis
- 2 pts: Defensible but broad thesis
- 1 pt: Completion/effort/grade claim with little reasoning focus
- 0 pts: No controlling claim, or pure self-praise without a pattern

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require a particular growth theme from the model list.
- Do not require the word “pattern” if a specific growth claim is clear.
- Do not withhold credit for honest modest claims; perfection claims are the problem, not humility.

## Question 6: Part 2 Q4 – Warrants and pattern (3 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** States why each artifact’s feature counts as evidence for the thesis (warrants) and explains why the three cases show a pattern of growth rather than isolated correct answers.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Warrants may be principles such as “disciplined thinkers size claims to evidence,” “revise when assumptions fail,” “test sources before trust,” “clarify terms before disputing,” etc. Pattern language may stress recurrence, improvement over time, transfer across contexts, or cumulative habit strength. Compact parallel structure is fine.
- **Required meaning:** Explicit bridges from features to thesis **and** a cross-artifact pattern claim. Restating the thesis without warrants, or listing artifacts without a growth pattern, fails full credit.

**Rubric:**
- 3 pts: Explicit warrants and convincing cross-artifact pattern
- 2 pts: Warrants mostly sound but pattern underexplained, or pattern clear but warrants thin for one case
- 1 pt: Repeats thesis without bridge, or lists artifacts without pattern
- 0 pts: No evidentiary reasoning

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require three separately labeled “Warrant 1/2/3” headings if each artifact is clearly bridged.
- Do not require the model’s exact warrant sentences.
- Do not accept “I got these right” as a growth warrant.

## Question 7: Part 3 Q1 – Counterpressure and qualification (3 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Names a genuine remaining weakness, contrary artifact, or fair objection; then qualifies the thesis so it remains accurate and still meaningful (not erased, not pretending the counterpressure is irrelevant).
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Counterpressure: uneven habit, one weak week, possible grade-driven revision, limited sample of artifacts, remaining overclaim, etc. Qualification: “more often,” “in claim-sizing more than in source testing,” “when I slow down,” “not yet automatic,” etc.
- **Required meaning:** Fair counterpressure **and** a substantive qualification that preserves a non-empty thesis. Token “I still make mistakes” with no thesis adjustment is partial.

**Rubric:**
- 3 pts: Fair counterpressure and substantive, preserving qualification
- 2 pts: One element strong and the other thin
- 1 pt: Token admission with no thesis change, or qualification that empties the claim
- 0 pts: No test of the defense

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the counterpressure to refute the entire thesis; a real pressure point is enough.
- Do not reward self-denigration that abandons all growth claims.
- Do not require a specific form of qualification language.
