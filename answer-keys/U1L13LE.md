# U1L13LE – Lit Example Worksheet Answer Key & Rubric

**Total Points:** 16

**Paired reader:** `units/elements-of-logic-week13/lit-examples/U1L13LE_lit_example_reader.tex` / `.pdf`.

**Paired worksheet:** `graded-assignments/U1L13LE/worksheet.tex` / `.pdf` (Assignment ID **U1L13LE**).

**Purpose:** Students apply Week 13 testimony / credibility-map diagnosis to the witches in *Macbeth* and Brutus in *Julius Caesar*.

## Grading Vocabulary for This Key

- **Testimony:** a claim offered by a source, witness, speaker, or record.
- **Credibility:** the degree to which a source deserves trust in this case.
- **Access:** whether the source was in a position to know the claim.
- **Bias / pressure:** a motive, interest, fear, ambition, or need for self-defense that may tilt testimony.
- **Corroboration:** independent support from events, evidence, or other sources.
- **Careful conclusion:** a trust judgment stated at the right strength rather than all-or-nothing trust or rejection.

Plot summary alone does not earn points. Quotation is permitted but not required. Credit students who avoid all-or-nothing answers.

## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Witches' claims and corroboration (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** The witches hail/call Macbeth Thane of Glamis, Thane of Cawdor, and future king. Macbeth already is Glamis; the surprising claim soon corroborated (by Ross/news from the king) is that Macbeth has been named Thane of Cawdor. The kingship claim remains a prediction, not yet confirmed.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Claims: “Glamis, Cawdor, and king hereafter” or clear paraphrase. Corroborated: “Cawdor,” “he becomes Thane of Cawdor,” “Ross confirms Cawdor.” Students may note Glamis was already true (not a new surprise).
- **Required meaning:** Identify the witches’ main claims about Macbeth and mark **Cawdor** (not kingship) as the soon-corroborated claim. Treating kingship as already corroborated fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Names the claims and identifies Cawdor as the soon-corroborated claim
- 1 pt: Names some claims but unclear corroboration, or gets Cawdor right without clear claim list
- 0 pts: Wrong claims, treats kingship as already proved, pure plot dump, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require naming Ross if “soon confirmed / news arrives” is clear.
- Do not require quotation of the hail.
- Do not require analysis of motive on this item.

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – Why corroboration tempts Macbeth (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** The Cawdor confirmation makes the witches tempting because they appear to know hidden or future information Macbeth did not know. One accurate and unexpected detail increases the psychological force / apparent credibility of the rest of their testimony.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “they knew something secret,” “one prediction came true so the next seems likely,” “makes them seem prophetic/reliable,” “encourages him to believe he will be king,” “harder to dismiss them as nonsense.”
- **Required meaning:** Explain **why** the corroborated detail increases temptation/apparent credibility—not merely that Cawdor came true. Link accuracy of one claim to trust in the remaining claims.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Explains why accurate hidden/unexpected knowledge increases apparent credibility or temptation
- 1 pt: Says only that one prediction came true, with little why
- 0 pts: No link to temptation/credibility, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require psychological jargon.
- Do not require claiming Macbeth is right to obey them.
- Ordinary language is enough.

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – What remains uncertain (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** The kingship claim remains uncorroborated. The witches’ source of knowledge, moral trustworthiness, meaning, and purpose remain uncertain. Cawdor being true does not prove that Macbeth must become king, should seek the crown, or should trust every implication of their speech.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “he is not king yet,” “kingship unproved,” “don’t know if they tell truth overall,” “unclear motives,” “words may be tricks,” “Cawdor ≠ must murder / must be king,” “how they know is unknown.”
- **Required meaning:** Identify remaining uncertainty after Cawdor—especially kingship and/or trustworthiness/meaning/purpose. Treating Cawdor as proof of everything fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Identifies uncorroborated kingship and/or clear limits of meaning, source, or trust after Cawdor
- 1 pt: Notes only that Macbeth is not king yet, with little further limit
- 0 pts: Treats Cawdor as proof of everything, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require all model uncertainties; kingship unproved plus one further limit (motive, meaning, or “should not act”) is strong.
- Do not require later-play knowledge (Birnam Wood, etc.) unless used carefully as what remains unknown **at this moment**.

## Question 4: Part 2 Q2 – Careful conclusion about witches (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Macbeth has reason to take the witches seriously because one claim is corroborated, but he should not treat them as fully trustworthy guides. Confirmed Cawdor supports attention, not obedience or certainty about kingship.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Balanced judgments such as “partly credible / not fully trust,” “listen but verify,” “Cawdor earns attention; kingship still open,” “dangerous to obey,” “take seriously without certainty.” Probability language: “some reason to credit,” “not enough to settle,” “tempting but unproved.”
- **Required meaning:** A careful (non-absolute) trust judgment with some credibility reason. Bare “trust them” or “ignore them” with no analysis fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Gives a balanced trust judgment with reason (probability / partial-credit language OK)
- 1 pt: Overtrusts or dismisses them but includes some reason
- 0 pts: Says simply “trust them” or “ignore them” with no credibility analysis, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require a fixed formula sentence.
- Do not require formal terms “corroboration” or “credibility” if the balance is clear.
- Do not punish students who stress danger of the witches if they still credit the Cawdor point appropriately.

## Question 5: Part 3 Q1 – Brutus's claim and access (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Brutus testifies that Caesar was ambitious and that killing him served Rome’s freedom more than Brutus’s personal love for Caesar. Brutus has access because he knew Caesar, was a Roman political actor, and participated in the decision; he has some competence as a public man judging political danger.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Claim: “Caesar was ambitious,” “Caesar threatened liberty,” “I killed him for Rome not hate,” “Caesar’s death was for the common good.” Access/competence: knew Caesar, senator/conspirator role, present for events, public leader, close friend who could observe ambition.
- **Required meaning:** Both central claim about Caesar (ambition/danger to Rome) and relevant access or competence. Claim alone or access alone is incomplete.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: States central claim and relevant access/competence
- 1 pt: States claim or access only, or both thinly
- 0 pts: Wrong claim focus, pure plot summary, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require quoting the funeral speech.
- Do not require deciding whether Brutus is right on this item.
- Accept “he was there / he knew Caesar” as access without a long biography.

## Question 6: Part 3 Q2 – Brutus's pressure and needed corroboration (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Brutus has strong pressure to justify an assassination he helped commit and to preserve his honor before the crowd. Corroboration would include specific public actions by Caesar, independent witnesses, laws or records, Caesar’s own words, or evidence that Caesar intended tyranny. Antony’s later counterexamples would also test Brutus’s testimony.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Pressure/bias: self-justification, honor, fear of looking like a murderer, political survival, persuasion of the crowd, need to seem noble. Corroboration: Caesar’s deeds (crown refusals debate, Lupercal, appointments), independent witnesses, documents, pattern of tyranny, other senators’ evidence; testing against Antony’s counterevidence.
- **Required meaning:** Both (1) bias/pressure affecting testimony and (2) specific corroboration that would strengthen the claim. Treating Brutus’s honor alone as enough proof fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Names pressure/bias and specific corroborating evidence (or clear test)
- 1 pt: Mentions bias or evidence only
- 0 pts: Treats Brutus’s honor as enough proof, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require accusing Brutus of lying; incentive to justify is enough.
- Do not require a full Antony speech analysis; noting counterevidence or need for Caesar’s public acts is enough.
- One clear pressure + one clear corroboration path earns full credit.

## Question 7: Part 4 Q1 – Comparing source credibility (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Compare both sources without flattening them. The witches have one startlingly corroborated detail, but purpose and reliability remain suspect. Brutus is honorable and close to events, but he is defending his own violent act and gives limited evidence in the speech. One accurate detail or one honorable speaker can increase credibility, but neither settles every claim without relevant corroboration.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Any paragraph that (a) treats both witches and Brutus and (b) explains why one accurate detail or one honorable speaker is not enough by itself. Students may judge one relatively stronger if they still state the limit. Ordinary language OK.
- **Required meaning:** Comparison of both sources + the lesson that partial credibility (one detail / honor) does not settle the whole question. Plot summary only fails. Discussing only one source fails full credit.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Compares both sources and explains why credibility remains limited / one detail or honor is not enough
- 1 pt: Discusses one source well or gives a thin comparison
- 0 pts: Plot summary only, all-or-nothing with no reason, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require equal paragraph space for both sources if both appear and the limit is clear.
- Do not require declaring a single “winner” source.
- Do not require formal map labels on this item (Part 4 Q2 is the map).

## Question 8: Part 4 Q2 – Credibility map (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer (either source):**
  - **Witches map:** claim (Cawdor / kingship); source (witches); access (strange knowledge of Cawdor); bias/pressure (unclear motive, tempting ambition); corroboration (Ross/news confirms Cawdor; kingship not confirmed); careful conclusion (attend but do not fully trust / do not treat kingship as settled).
  - **Brutus map:** claim (Caesar was ambitious / dangerous to liberty); source (Brutus); access (knew Caesar / public role); bias/pressure (must justify assassination / honor before crowd); corroboration/conflict (needs Caesar’s actions, records; Antony’s counterevidence); careful conclusion (plausible but not settled by the speech alone).
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Students choose one source. Labels may vary (ordinary words for access, bias, etc.). Careful conclusion must be non-absolute and tied to the map elements.
- **Required meaning:** A coherent map covering claim, source, access, bias, corroboration or conflict, and careful conclusion for the chosen source. Summary or unsupported trust judgment fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Completes a coherent credibility map with careful conclusion for one source
- 1 pt: Includes some elements but incomplete (missing major pieces)
- 0 pts: Summary or unsupported trust judgment, wrong source with no map, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require both sources on this item; one complete map is enough.
- Do not require identical label words if the six jobs are clearly done.
- Do not require quotation.
- A list or short paragraph form both earn full credit when complete.

**Overall Notes for Grading This Worksheet:**
- Whole points only. Total: 8 × 2 = 16 points.
- Credit students who avoid all-or-nothing answers. Week 13 asks how far testimony deserves trust, not whether every source is either perfect or worthless.
- Do not require exact model wording; preserve credibility distinctions and text grounding.
