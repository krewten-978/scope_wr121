## Grading Vocabulary for This Key

- **Circular reasoning / begging the question:** the offered reason depends on already accepting the conclusion, including when it restates the conclusion in different words.
- **Independent support:** a reason or evidence that can be evaluated without first granting the conclusion.
- **Loaded definition:** a definition that builds the disputed verdict into a key term.
- **Restatement:** the conclusion repeated in other words and offered as a reason.
- **Rewrite:** a non-circular version with testable support or a claim narrowed to what the evidence establishes.
- **Shifting term / equivocation:** Week 8 review — one word used in two contextually different senses.
- **Fallacy defect:** the point where apparent support fails to establish the conclusion.

## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Efficient schedule (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** The reason is circular because calling the schedule “the most efficient schedule available” already assumes the disputed conclusion that it is efficient. No independent measure, comparison, result, or evidence is offered; *efficient* is repeated in stronger wording and treated as its own proof.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “it says efficient because efficient,” “most efficient already assumes it is efficient,” “the reason just restates the claim,” or an explanation that there are no stated criteria such as time saved, cost, conflicts avoided, or comparison with alternatives.
- **Required meaning:** The response must explain that the reason presupposes or repeats the conclusion rather than supporting it independently. Merely saying that the argument is weak is incomplete.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Clearly explains that “most efficient” assumes/restates “efficient” and therefore supplies no independent support
- 1 pt: Correctly says the argument is circular, repetitive, or unsupported but does not explain where the repetition/assumption occurs
- 0 pts: Says the argument is strong because “most efficient” proves efficient, gives an unrelated schedule objection, or gives no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the terms “circular reasoning,” “begging the question,” “restatement,” or “independent support.”
- Do not require the student to propose replacement evidence; the prompt asks only why this reason is circular.
- A concise explanation such as “it uses efficiency as the proof of efficiency” earns full credit.

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – Trustworthy source (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Independent support would be concrete evidence for the source's reliability that does not merely rename it trustworthy—for example, a relevant record of accurate reporting, transparent and checkable methods, corroboration by other evidence or sources, relevant expertise, disclosed and examined conflicts of interest, or specific verifiable evidence in the report.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Other answers may earn full credit if they name at least one observable fact, method, comparison, credential relevant to the claim, or corroborating source that could be checked without first assuming trustworthiness.
- **Required meaning:** The answer must propose a specific kind of evidence that could independently support trusting the source or report. The worksheet asks what support would look like, not for a complete source investigation or a fixed number of reasons.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Gives at least one concrete, relevant, independently checkable basis for trusting the source or report
- 1 pt: Says “more evidence,” “proof,” “research,” or “check the source” without specifying what evidence or check would bear on reliability
- 0 pts: Repeats that the source is trustworthy/reliable because it can be trusted, appeals only to the source's own label, or gives no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require multiple supports, all model examples, or the exact phrase “independent support.” One well-specified support satisfies the prompt.
- Relevant expertise must fit the report's subject; status or fame alone is not automatically independent support.
- “Other trustworthy people agree” is insufficient unless the response identifies genuine independent corroboration rather than repeating the same unsupported trust label.

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – Serious student (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** The conclusion that the student is “not serious” is assumed in the first premise's loaded definition: it defines a serious student as someone who never questions the rubric. Once that definition is granted, questioning the rubric automatically produces the conclusion without independent evidence about the student's seriousness.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “the first sentence assumes it,” “never questioning is unfairly built into *serious student*,” “it defines anyone who questions as not serious,” or comparable language that locates the assumption in the stated criterion.
- **Required meaning:** The response must identify where the conclusion is built in and explain that questioning a rubric has been treated as the definition or automatic test of seriousness.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Locates the assumption in the first premise/definition and explains how “never questions the rubric” predetermines the not-serious conclusion
- 1 pt: Points to “serious student” or calls the definition unfair/circular but does not clearly explain how it assumes the conclusion
- 0 pts: Debates whether questioning rubrics is good without locating the assumed conclusion, accepts the definition as proof, or gives no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the terms “loaded definition,” “criterion,” “premise,” or “begging the question.”
- The prompt asks where the conclusion is assumed; a correct answer must point to the definition/first sentence, not merely label the whole argument circular.
- Do not require an alternative definition of a serious student.

## Question 4: Part 3 Q1 – Fair election rule rewrite (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical model with independent support:** “This election rule is fair because it gives every eligible voter the same access, uses published procedures, and is administered without a conflicting interest.”
- **Canonical model with a narrowed claim:** “This rule gives every eligible voter the same voting period, so it is fairer than the old rule on equal access.”
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Many rewrites are possible. The conclusion may remain “the rule is fair” if the reason supplies a concrete, testable fairness criterion or evidence. Alternatively, the student may narrow the conclusion to a specific supported comparison or feature. The wording need not match the models.
- **Required meaning:** The rewrite must retain a clear conclusion and must replace “fair because fair elections use it” with either independent support or a genuinely narrower claim supported by a stated feature. It must not hide the original conclusion in synonyms alone.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Gives a complete non-circular rewrite with a clear conclusion and either concrete independent support or a supported narrowed claim
- 1 pt: Improves the argument but leaves the support vague, leaves the conclusion unclear, or narrows the claim without stating what feature supports it
- 0 pts: Still argues “fair because fair/right/good,” merely restates the original, supplies no conclusion, or gives no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Treat the models as examples, not required content. Equal access, transparent procedures, neutral administration, ballot secrecy, consistent counting, comparison data, or another relevant criterion may work.
- Do not require a specific number of reasons. One sufficiently concrete independent reason satisfies the worksheet.
- A different political judgment is acceptable if the logical rewrite is non-circular and the stated evidence actually bears on its conclusion.

## Question 5: Part 4 Q1 – Stadium lights paragraph (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** **Main conclusion:** the stadium lights project must be approved. **Circular/assumption-loaded reason:** the project should be approved because it is “the project this community needs,” real supporters “already know” the lights are necessary, or questioners are defined as non-supporters; these formulations assume necessity or support instead of proving it. **One better independent support:** for example, documented safety needs, actual usage hours, a cost and benefit comparison, budget tradeoffs including the library roof, maintenance records, energy estimates, or evidence of how many activities require lights.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept faithful paraphrases of the approval conclusion, any one of the printed circular/loaded reasons with an explanation of how it presupposes necessity or dismisses the proof burden, and any one relevant, checkable support that would help decide whether to approve the project.
- **Required meaning:** All three components explicitly requested by the prompt must appear: conclusion, circular reason, and one better independent support. The better support need not prove approval by itself, but it must be relevant and independently assessable.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Accurately identifies the approval conclusion, a printed reason that assumes necessity/support, and one concrete relevant independent support
- 1 pt: Accurately supplies two of the three required components, or gives all three with one materially vague
- 0 pts: Supplies one or no correct components, focuses on the projector or speaker sitting down, repeats “the community needs it” as independent proof, or gives no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require all circular phrases or more than one proposed support.
- The library-roof cost question may be used as part of a valid budget comparison; it is not automatically a distractor because competing priorities are relevant to project approval.
- “Only non-supporters ask for proof” is useful only if the student explains that the label dismisses critics by assuming what a real supporter must believe.

## Question 6: Part 5 Q1 – Review: bats (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** The shifting term is **bat/bats**: first a flying mammal or animal, then a baseball bat or other piece of sports equipment ordered by the coach.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “cave animal” versus “something used to hit a ball,” “animal” versus “baseball/cricket equipment,” or comparable context-fitting descriptions.
- **Required meaning:** The response must identify the repeated term and both distinct meanings.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Identifies bat/bats and clearly gives both animal and sports-equipment senses
- 0 pts: Gives only the term, gives only one sense, gives contextually wrong senses, or gives no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require “equivocation,” “ambiguous middle,” or “flying mammal.”
- Singular/plural variation does not matter.

## Question 7: Part 5 Q2 – Review: spirit week (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** **Conclusion:** the person who questioned the spirit-week schedule is against school pride. **Main defect:** the first premise falsely, doubtfully, or without support equates questioning one schedule with opposing school pride; the loyalty framing pressures the hearer but does not establish that equation.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Accept “questioning the schedule does not mean being against pride,” “unsupported assumption/generalization,” “false dilemma,” or “unfair leap” when the response clearly identifies the failed bridge. Ordinary language is sufficient.
- **Required meaning:** The response must state the actual conclusion and explain the central defect. Merely naming loyalty pressure or “appearance of proof” without saying why the support fails is incomplete.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Gives the against-school-pride conclusion and accurately explains the unsupported equation between schedule criticism and opposition to school pride
- 0 pts: Omits the conclusion or defect, merely says there is loyalty pressure, calls the printed if-then form invalid without locating the premise problem, or gives no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the terms “bad inference,” “false premise,” “appearance of proof,” or “false dilemma.”
- A student may call the everyday move a bad inference if the explanation accurately identifies the unsupported bridge. The pressure itself is not the full defect unless the student explains what it fails to prove.
