## Grading Vocabulary for This Key

- **Revision trail:** original claim, precise diagnosis, substantive revision, and reflective caption.
- **Substantive revision:** a change to the reasoning, not merely style or proofreading.
- **Criterion:** the logical standard that tests the original claim.
- **Equivocation / term test:** clarification of an ambiguous promise and whether a preferred sense was chosen without testing.
- **Source credibility:** whether the apparitions/witches warrant the confidence Macbeth draws.
- **Possibility-to-necessity move:** treating what might happen as sufficient proof that one action must happen.
- **Serpent's-egg analogy:** comparing Caesar to an egg that will grow mischievous if hatched; supports a warning only if the fit and alternatives are tested.

Plot summary alone does not earn points. Quotation is optional unless the student needs it to make a point clear. Accept different fitting Whately tools when the passage-grounded diagnosis is precise.

## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Macbeth model: notice and oversimplification (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** The early claim notices that Macbeth is dangerously misled / fooled by the apparitions. It oversimplifies by treating the apparition’s words as a straightforward falsehood (“the witches lie”). The promise is equivocal and later gets a technical fulfillment; the key failure includes Macbeth’s preferred interpretation and inference to practical certainty.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Notice: Macbeth is deceived, overconfident, misled, or dangerously secure. Oversimplification: calling the words a plain lie; missing ambiguity/equivocation; missing that the wording can be technically true later; blaming only the witches and ignoring Macbeth’s inference. Ordinary language is fine.
- **Required meaning:** Both jobs—(1) what the early claim correctly notices **and** (2) what it oversimplifies about the apparition’s words. Plot summary or “witches are bad” alone fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Names both valid notice and precise oversimplification
- 1 pt: One side only, or both thin
- 0 pts: Plot summary only, pure moralizing, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the words “equivocal” or “technical fulfillment” if ambiguity and oversimplification of “lie” are clear.
- Do not require naming Macduff or the later birth twist if the student correctly flags “lie” as too simple for the promise’s wording.
- Do not accept “Macbeth is evil” as the notice/oversimplification pair.

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – Macbeth model: revised judgment (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Revised judgment: Macbeth accepts the most reassuring sense of an ambiguous promise, fails to test terms/source, infers security, and uses that confidence while choosing further violence. Stronger than “the witches lie” because it names textual evidence, the inferential failure, and relevant tools rather than treating every word as a simple falsehood.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Revised judgment may emphasize any coherent cluster: preferred sense of “none of woman born”; failure to clarify terms; weak source test; leap from promise to practical certainty; confidence used to justify killing Macduff / “assurance double sure.” Why stronger: more precise; names evidence + inference + action; distinguishes equivocal wording from Macbeth’s chosen reading; applies a course habit.
- **Required meaning:** A revised judgment in the model’s direction **and** a reason it improves on “the witches lie.” Repeating the early claim, or a revised idea with no comparison, is incomplete.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Accurate revised judgment plus why it improves the original
- 1 pt: Sound revised idea without explanation, or explanation without a clear revised judgment
- 0 pts: Repeats the early claim, plot only, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the student to reproduce the model paragraph word for word.
- Do not require every model detail (sense + source + violence) if the core improvement—ambiguous promise → chosen certainty, not mere “lie”—is clear and explained.
- Do not require formal tool names if the logical improvement is explained.

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – Macbeth evidence, inference, action (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Evidence: “none of woman born / Shall harm Macbeth” (the apparition’s promise). Inference: Macduff need not be feared / Macbeth is secure from ordinary human harm (“what need I fear of thee?”). Action: make assurance double sure / have Macduff killed (“thou shalt not live”). Fitting habit: term clarification/equivocation, source credibility, hidden-assumption test, or inference/claim test.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Evidence may be paraphrased as the woman-born promise or security promise. Inference: no fear of Macduff; safety; invulnerability to men. Action: kill Macduff; double assurance; bond of fate. Habit: any one fitting name or clear ordinary description of the test (clarify the key phrase; test the source; check what the promise actually proves).
- **Required meaning:** All three roles—evidence, inference, action—**and** one fitting habit that tests the inference. Plot-only answers fail.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: All three roles plus a fitting habit
- 1 pt: Two of three roles clear, or three roles without a fitting habit, or habit named without clear application to the map
- 0 pts: Plot only, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require quotation if accurate paraphrase identifies the promise, the security inference, and the added violence.
- Do not require multiple habits; one well-aimed habit is enough.
- Do not require a closed official tool list if the described test fits the inference.

## Question 4: Part 2 Q2 – Macbeth reflective caption (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Caption covers: original (promise treated as a lie / witches fool Macbeth); diagnosis (oversimplification; missed inference from ambiguous promise to certainty); substantive change (distinguish equivocal wording from Macbeth’s chosen interpretation and action); what the change shows (more precise term, source, and/or inference judgment).
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Compact paragraph or bullets. Order may vary. Growth language may stress disciplined reading, testing promises, or not collapsing ambiguity into “lie.”
- **Required meaning:** Original, diagnosis, substantive change, and growth-about-judgment all visible enough to reconstruct the trail. General praise of the revision fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Original, diagnosis, change, and growth all visible
- 1 pt: Sound but missing one major element
- 0 pts: General praise, plot summary only, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require five labels if the four prompt jobs are present.
- Do not require the exact model wording.
- Honest limited growth language is acceptable.

## Question 5: Part 3 Q1 – Brutus early-response diagnosis (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** “Brutus cares about Rome, so his decision is logical” confuses honorable motive with adequate proof. Caring may explain motive but does not prove the conclusion. Precise weaknesses include: gap from “might change” to “must be by his death”; serpent’s-egg analogy’s uncertain fit; no present personal cause yet preventive death; untested alternatives. Any one exact, passage-grounded weakness with a fitting criterion earns full credit.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Criteria/ordinary equivalents: hidden assumption; possibility-to-necessity; analogy/disanalogy; evidence proportion; missing-the-point; form vs fact; insufficient support; alternatives unexamined. Weakness statements may focus on one clear gap. Passage anchors: might change; must be by his death; serpent’s egg; no personal cause; for the general.
- **Required meaning:** One exact weakness grounded in the passage **and** a fitting logical criterion/test (named or clearly described). Praising or condemning Brutus without logic fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Exact passage-grounded weakness with fitting criterion
- 1 pt: Correct concern stated generally, or criterion without a specific passage feature
- 0 pts: Praises or condemns Brutus without logic, plot only, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the student to list every possible weakness; one precise diagnosis is enough.
- Do not require a formal criterion label if the logical test is clear and passage-tied.
- Do not require quotation; accurate reference to might→must, the egg analogy, or missing present cause is enough.

## Question 6: Part 3 Q2 – Revised Brutus judgment (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** A revised judgment preserves that Brutus expresses genuine concern for Rome / the general good, but judges that concern insufficient to prove necessary death. The possibility of corruption, supported by an analogy, still requires evidence of likely tyranny, a defense of the analogy’s fit, and/or examination of less extreme alternatives.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Revisions that: keep motive/concern as real; reject motive-as-proof; mark might≠must; limit the egg analogy to a warning not a proof of required killing; demand more evidence or alternatives. Wording need not match the model.
- **Required meaning:** Preserves what the passage supports **and** tests the move from possible danger to necessary death. Mere plot retell or pure moral verdict (“Brutus is wrong/right”) fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Balanced, precise revision that tests possibility-to-necessity (or an equivalent precise burden)
- 1 pt: Directionally correct but thin, or preserves concern without testing necessity (or tests necessity while erasing all supported concern)
- 0 pts: Mere plot, moral verdict only, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require both “defend the analogy” and “examine alternatives” if one clear test of the necessity leap is present with preserved concern.
- Do not require the student to decide Caesar would or would not become a tyrant.
- Do not accept a revision that still equates caring about Rome with logical proof of necessary death.

## Question 7: Part 4 Q1 – Brutus reflective caption (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Caption names: (1) original limit (motive treated as logic / concern proves the decision); (2) Whately criterion (e.g. hidden assumption, analogy, evidence proportion, possibility-to-necessity); (3) textual change (adds uncertainty of “might,” burden for “must,” analogy limits, or missing alternatives); (4) stronger judgment now possible (honorable motive ≠ adequate proof of necessary death).
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Ordinary-language criterion descriptions. Textual change may be described as what the revised judgment now includes or refuses. Compact structure OK.
- **Required meaning:** All four prompt jobs—original limit, criterion, textual change, stronger judgment. General reflection without those jobs fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Complete evidence-based revision caption with all four elements
- 1 pt: Missing one major element, or all present but one thin
- 0 pts: General reflection only, plot only, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require a formal course criterion name if the logical test is clearly named/described.
- Do not require long quotation; reference to the key textual move is enough.
- Do not require a specific single official criterion if the one used fits the diagnosis.

## Question 8: Part 4 Q2 – Compare revision trails (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Both revision trails slow the move from powerful language or honorable motive to certainty/necessity. Shared habits include clarifying terms, inspecting sources/analogies, and testing the inferential bridge against textual evidence. The portfolio connection names one real Shakespeare artifact and one concrete improvement using that habit.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Shared habit phrasing may vary: test inferences; don’t treat motive as proof; clarify ambiguous promises; size claims to evidence; distinguish force/rhetoric from proof; check analogies. Portfolio application may name any recoverable Shakespeare-related course artifact and a specific improvement (narrower claim, term test, analogy limit, etc.).
- **Required meaning:** (1) A recurring habit both Macbeth and Brutus trails make visible **and** (2) how that habit could improve one Shakespeare portfolio artifact. Plot comparison only, or habit without portfolio application, is incomplete.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Shared habit visible in both trails plus concrete portfolio application
- 1 pt: Sound comparison without portfolio application, or portfolio application without a clear shared habit, or habit tied to only one passage
- 0 pts: Plot comparison only, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require equal paragraph length for Macbeth and Brutus.
- Do not require a formal assignment ID if the portfolio artifact is otherwise recoverable.
- Do not require the student to use the word “habit” if the recurring disciplined-reading move is clear.
- Both passages must appear for full credit on the comparison side; the portfolio piece is a third concrete application.
