# U1L17LL – Logic Lab Answer Key & Rubric

**Total Points:** 16

**Paired worksheet:** `graded-assignments/U1L17LL/worksheet.tex` / `.pdf` (Assignment ID **U1L17LL**).

**Paired reading:** Week 17 — self-deception, motivated reasoning, rationalization, private argument vs public cover, and multi-tool autopsy.

## Grading Vocabulary for This Key

- **Self-deception:** persuading yourself of what you already want to believe.
- **Motivated reasoning:** desire chooses the conclusion first; reasons follow.
- **Rationalization:** reasons invented after the choice to protect the self.
- **Private argument / public cover:** inner justification versus cleaner outer story.
- **Hidden assumption:** unstated premise the argument needs.
- **Self-deception autopsy:** desire; conclusion; stated reasons; hidden assumption; exposing tool; remaining need; repaired narrower claim.
- **Review terms:** Week 16 rhetoric-vs-proof; Week 15 real dispute after shared definition.

Week 17 rewards multi-tool autopsy and narrow repaired claims without cheap cynicism about every motive. Grade autopsy **jobs**, not one exclusive tool name, when the diagnostic work is precise. Formal course tool names are welcome but not uniquely required when the prompt says “name one Whately tool” and the student names a clear, fitting prior habit.

## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Bench starter (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Desire/feared loss: keep starting role / avoid shame of the bench. Conclusion wanted: I still deserve to start (coach is unfair, so the consequence should not apply).
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Desire: keep starting, avoid bench, protect status, avoid shame. Conclusion: I deserve to start anyway; coach is unfair so I should start; missed practices shouldn’t cost my spot. Ordinary language is fine.
- **Required meaning:** Desire or feared loss **and** the wanted conclusion. Moralizing about the player without those two jobs fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Names desire and conclusion
- 1 pt: One element only
- 0 pts: Only moralizes about the player, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require both “desire” and “feared loss” labels; one clear motivator is enough.
- Do not require formal “motivated reasoning.”
- Do not require judging whether the coach is actually unfair.

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – Novel procrastination (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Desire: avoid starting now / protect comfort or ego. Conclusion: waiting until tonight is smart planning because “I work better under pressure.”
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Desire: delay work, comfort, avoid hard start, protect self-image as a “pressure worker.” Conclusion: waiting is smart; pressure improves work; tonight is better planning. Ordinary paraphrases fine.
- **Required meaning:** Desire **and** conclusion. “Procrastination is bad” alone fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Names desire and conclusion
- 1 pt: Thin or partial
- 0 pts: Only says procrastination is bad, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require diagnosing whether the student truly works better under pressure.
- Do not require formal labels.
- Compact answers are fine.

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – Group project method (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Hidden assumption: hours invested prove comparative quality, or sunk effort entitles method-dominance. Exposing tools: missing-the-point, weak induction, form-vs-fact, hidden-assumption test — time spent does not entail “best method.”
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Assumption: more hours = better method; sunk cost proves quality; effort entitles control; my time investment means my way is best. Tools: hidden assumption, weak evidence, correlation/causation, missing the point, induction limits, form vs fact, irrelevant proof. Any one fitting tool name or clear tool description.
- **Required meaning:** Surface the hidden assumption **and** name one exposing tool. Agreeing the method is best because of hours fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Surfaces assumption and names a fitting tool
- 1 pt: One element only
- 0 pts: Agrees the method is best because of hours, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require a specific official tool from a closed list if the diagnostic work fits.
- Do not require both “hours = quality” and “sunk cost” formulations; one clear assumption is enough.
- Ordinary description of the tool is acceptable.

## Question 4: Part 2 Q2 – Shortcut friend (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Weak move: loyalty/ethos of friendship treated as proof of safety expertise; possible irrelevant authority. Credibility/source tool: access, relevant expertise, bias, independent corroboration — friendship is not safety evidence.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Weak move: friend loyalty ≠ safety proof; irrelevant authority; ethos used as logos; missing expertise. Tool: source test, credibility test, testimony test, authority check, corroboration, bias/access/expertise map. One clear weak move + one fitting tool.
- **Required meaning:** Surface the weak move **and** name the credibility/proof tool that tests it. Accepting friend loyalty as safety proof fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Names weak move and credibility/proof test
- 1 pt: Partial
- 0 pts: Accepts friend loyalty as safety proof, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the full credibility checklist (access/expertise/bias/corroboration); naming the right kind of tool is enough.
- Do not require formal “irrelevant authority” if the friendship-vs-safety mismatch is clear.
- Ordinary language is fine.

## Question 5: Part 3 Q1 – Club title (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Private argument: I want the title for applications/status. Public cover: continuity for the club. Independent support needed for the cover: evidence the club actually needs this person for continuity, that alternatives are weaker, and that continuity—not résumé—is the governing aim.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Private: résumé, college apps, status, power. Public: club needs me, continuity, stability, service. Needed support: evidence of real continuity need, unique fitness, alternatives considered, public reason not just private gain. One clear independent-support burden is enough.
- **Required meaning:** Private argument, public cover, **and** what independent support the cover would need. Treating the public cover as automatically true fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Separates private/public and names needed support
- 1 pt: Partial separation (missing support or one side of private/public)
- 0 pts: Treats public cover as automatically true, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the model’s full support checklist; one concrete independent-support need is enough with private/public split.
- Do not require condemning every ambition; the task is separation + support burden.
- Ordinary language is fine.

## Question 6: Part 3 Q2 – Texting driver (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Rationalization: shifts blame to “anyone would” and to stoplights, protecting the self from responsibility. Repaired claim: I looked down at my phone while driving; that increased risk — regardless of stoplight design.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Rationalization: everyone does it; blame the lights; minimize personal agency; excuse. Repaired claim: I texted/looked at my phone while driving; that was risky; I chose to look down; stoplights don’t erase my action. Fact-closer, narrower, responsibility-keeping repairs.
- **Required meaning:** Name the rationalization **and** a repaired claim closer to the facts. Fully excusing the driver fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Names rationalization and a fact-closer repaired claim
- 1 pt: One element only
- 0 pts: Excuses the driver fully, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require both “anyone would” and “stoplights” in the rationalization diagnosis if one protective move is clear.
- Do not require perfect wording of the repaired claim; it must stay closer to the driver’s action/risk.
- “Don’t text and drive” as a slogan without claim-repair craft is weaker than a repaired first-person claim.

## Question 7: Part 4 Q1 – Play audition autopsy (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Desire: win the lead. Conclusion: I should get the part / if not, process was biased. Stated reasons: director likes confidence; nerves show deeper care; hard workers are rewarded; I work hard “in my head.” Hidden assumption: caring or private mental effort entitles the lead; outcome unfairness is default if denied. Exposing tools: circular/motivated reasoning, weak evidence, missing-the-point, equivocation on “hard work.” Still needs: actual audition criteria, comparative performance, director standards. Repaired claim: I want the lead and felt nervous because I care; that does not by itself prove I earned the part.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Ordinary-language autopsy covering the prompt’s seven jobs: desire; conclusion; stated reasons; hidden assumption; one exposing tool; what still needs judgment/evidence; repaired narrower claim. Tool names may vary if fitting. Repaired claim must drop false entitlement/necessity while allowing honest desire.
- **Required meaning:** Prompt lists seven autopsy jobs. Grade those jobs in substance. Only cheering or scolding Jordan fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Completes a coherent multi-part autopsy
- 1 pt: Several elements but incomplete
- 0 pts: Only cheers or scolds Jordan, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require every model stated-reason bullet if several reasons appear and other jobs are present.
- Do not require one official tool name if the exposing diagnosis is precise.
- Do not require the model’s full “still needs” checklist; one clear remaining evidence/judgment need is enough with other jobs present.
- Compact bullets or paragraph form is fine.

## Question 8: Part 5 Q1 – Review: campaign video (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Rhetorical force: pathos (music, torn flag) and ethos (“one of you”) pushing distrust/rejection. Unproved: the budget report’s content, whether rejection is justified, and whether identity belonging answers the policy question.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Force: emotion, belonging, identity ethos, distrust mood, pathos/ethos. Unproved: report is wrong/bad; voters should reject it; video answers the budget merits. Force-vs-unproved split required.
- **Required meaning:** Rhetorical force **and** what remains unproved. Treating the video as proof fails.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Separates force from unproved policy claim
- 0 pts: Treats the video as proof, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require both music and flag if emotional force is clear.
- Do not require formal pathos/ethos labels if force is described.
- One-point item: both sides of the split needed.

## Question 9: Part 5 Q2 – Review: fair punishment (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** After sharing a definition of fair, the remaining disagreement is **real**: it is about facts/application, not merely the word.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** “Real dispute,” “factual disagreement,” “about what happened / how the rule applies,” “not merely verbal anymore.”
- **Required meaning:** Classify the **remaining** disagreement as real (or clear equivalent) after shared definition. Calling it merely verbal despite shared definition fails.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Classifies remaining disagreement as real
- 0 pts: Calls it merely verbal despite shared definition, or no relevant answer

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require a full dispute map.
- Do not require settling the facts of the punishment.
- Formal “real” is preferred; clear equivalents OK.
