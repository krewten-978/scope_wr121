## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Route 9 buses mood (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
**A-A-A**, in the printed order: universal affirmative, universal affirmative, universal affirmative.

Accept “AAA,” “A, A, A,” or “A–A–A.” The prompt explicitly requests A/E/I/O letters for all three statements, so prose classifications without the three letters do not fully answer the question.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Supplies A for each of the three statements in the printed premise-premise-conclusion order.
- 0 pts: Omits a letter, gives any non-A letter, changes the order, or gives only a validity judgment.

**Notes for grading:** Do not require hyphens or an explanation. Do not require the student to label which A belongs to which statement when the three letters are clearly presented in order.

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – Summer reading mood (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
**O-E-O**, in the printed order: “Some novels are not…” is O; “No assigned summer reading…” is E; “Some assigned summer reading is not…” is O.

Accept “OEO,” “O, E, O,” or “O–E–O.”

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Supplies O, E, O in the order of the three printed statements.
- 0 pts: Omits a letter, gives any wrong letter, reverses the sequence, or answers only valid/invalid.

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require an explanation or a validity judgment; the prompt asks only for mood.
- Do not accept a reordered major/minor sequence. The worksheet asks for the three statements as presented, and the presented order is O-E-O.

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – Maples and oaks (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
Canonical answer: **formally invalid because of an undistributed middle**. “Trees” is the middle term, but each premise places a different class inside the broad class of trees; neither premise establishes that oaks are maples or that the two classes overlap in the required way.

Accept ordinary-language explanations such as “being trees does not make oaks maples,” “two groups can belong to the same larger group without being the same group,” or a valid counterexample in which maples and oaks are distinct kinds of trees. The technical phrase “undistributed middle” is not required because the prompt asks for a brief explanation, not a named fallacy.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Says the argument is invalid and explains the shared-broad-class defect, correctly identifies the middle term’s failure to connect oaks to maples, or gives a clear counterexample demonstrating that the premises can be true while the conclusion is false.
- 1 pt: Correctly says “invalid” but gives no reason or only a vague statement such as “the middle term is wrong” without explaining the missing connection.
- 0 pts: Says valid, gives a reason that does not address the form, or omits the judgment.

**Notes for grading:** Do not require “undistributed middle.” Do not award full credit for that phrase alone unless the response makes clear that “trees” does not connect membership in the oak class to membership in the maple class.

## Question 4: Part 2 Q2 – Podcasts and lectures (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
Canonical answer: **formally invalid because it has two negative premises**. The premises exclude both podcasts and classroom lectures from the class of live radio broadcasts, but they establish no relation between podcasts and classroom lectures. Those two classes could overlap or be separate while both premises remain true.

Accept explanations such as “sharing what they are not does not prove they are different from each other,” “both can be non-live-radio without being mutually exclusive,” or a counterexample in which a recorded classroom lecture is also a podcast and neither is a live radio broadcast.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Says invalid and explains that the two negative premises/common exclusion from live radio do not establish the claimed separation between podcasts and classroom lectures.
- 1 pt: Correctly says invalid but gives no adequate explanation, or identifies “two negative premises” without connecting that feature to the missing relation.
- 0 pts: Says valid, claims the conclusion follows merely because both premises are negative, or omits the judgment.

**Notes for grading:** Do not require the exact phrase “two negative premises” if the student accurately explains the logical gap. Do not require a counterexample; it is one acceptable way to explain the invalidity.

## Question 5: Part 3 Q1 – Senators and mayors (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
Canonical answer: **formally invalid because of illicit process of the major term**. The conclusion excludes every mayor from the entire class of elected officials, but the premises establish only that senators are elected officials and that mayors are not senators. A mayor could still be an elected official without being a senator.

Accept ordinary-language explanations such as “not being a senator does not mean not being elected,” “the conclusion makes a claim about all elected officials that the premises never establish,” or a counterexample involving an elected mayor who is not a senator. The terms “illicit process” and “distributed” are not required.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Says invalid and explains that the premises separate mayors from senators, not from all elected officials; an accurate illicit-major/distribution explanation or counterexample also earns full credit.
- 1 pt: Correctly says invalid but supplies no explanation, or names illicit process/distribution without showing what overreach occurs.
- 0 pts: Says valid, treats “not senator” as equivalent to “not elected official,” or omits the judgment.

**Notes for grading:** Do not require “illicit process of the major term.” If technical vocabulary is used, it must refer to “elected officials” being taken universally in the conclusion without being distributed in the relevant premise; a technical keyword attached to the wrong term does not earn explanation credit.

## Question 6: Part 4 Q1 – Priya paragraph (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
**Mood:** A-A-A, treating the two singular claims about Priya as universal affirmative propositions in the course’s categorical shorthand.
**Validity:** formally invalid as printed. The first premise applies only to finalists who received perfect scores, while the second states only that Priya received a perfect score; it never establishes that Priya is a finalist. Without that missing membership claim, Priya’s advancement does not follow.

Accept “AAA; invalid,” “A, A, A—not valid because Priya may not be a finalist,” or another concise response that supplies A-A-A and an invalidity judgment. A student who explicitly says the argument would become valid if the paragraph added “Priya is a finalist” has identified the same defect.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Supplies both requested components: A-A-A mood and invalid form. No explanation is required by the prompt, though an accurate missing-finalist explanation confirms the judgment.
- 1 pt: Supplies exactly one requested component correctly—either A-A-A or invalid—but omits or gets the other wrong.
- 0 pts: Supplies neither component correctly or says the form is valid without noticing that finalist status is absent.

**Notes for grading:** Do not import the unstated claim that Priya is a finalist from the debate setting, the bracket, or the key’s intended model. The printed paragraph establishes only a perfect score. If a student reports that the item appears intended to be valid but is missing “Priya is a finalist,” award full credit when the response also gives A-A-A. This is a content error in the former key/intended example, not an acceptable hidden premise.

## Question 7: Part 5 Q1 – Review: torque wrench terms (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Minor term:** the torque wrench.
- **Major term:** items/tools that must be returned to the cage, or the property “must be returned to the cage.”
- **Middle term:** tools in this kit / items in this kit.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Correctly names all three requested terms and assigns each to the right role. Accept the listed singular/plural and class/property phrasings when they preserve the conclusion’s subject, conclusion’s predicate, and the linking kit-membership class.
- 0 pts: Omits any term, swaps roles, or gives “tools” or “kit” alone so vaguely that the linking class cannot be identified.

**Notes for grading:** Do not require definitions or explanations; the prompt asks only to name the terms. Accept “torque wrench; must return to cage; tools in this kit” when the order or labels make the roles unambiguous.

## Question 8: Part 5 Q2 – Review: feathers light (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
The shifting term is **light**. In the first premise, “light” means bright, illuminated, or not dark. In the second premise, it means low in weight or not heavy. The conclusion does not follow because the argument changes meanings: feathers’ low weight does not establish that they are bright or unable to be dark.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Supplies all three explicitly requested components: identifies “light,” distinguishes brightness/illumination from low weight, and explains that substituting those meanings breaks the inference.
- 0 pts: Omits the shifting word, gives only one meaning, fails to distinguish the senses, or gives no explanation of why the conclusion does not follow.

**Notes for grading:** Do not require the formal word “equivocation.” Accept “bright versus not heavy,” “lit versus lightweight,” and similar ordinary-language pairs when the two senses are clear. A bare statement “light has two meanings” is incomplete because the prompt requires both meanings and an explanation.
