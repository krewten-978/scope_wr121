# U1L18LL – Logic Lab Answer Key & Rubric

**Total Points:** 20

**Paired worksheet:** `graded-assignments/U1L18LL/worksheet.tex` / `.pdf` (Assignment ID **U1L18LL**).

**Paired reading:** Week 18 — portfolio selection, criterion-based diagnosis, substantive revision, reflective captions, and transfer.

## Grading Vocabulary for This Key

- **Artifact:** a completed piece of course work that records reasoning at a particular moment.
- **Criterion:** a fitting logical standard used to judge a precise feature of reasoning.
- **Substantive revision:** a change to claim, evidence, inference, assumption, scope, or judgment—not merely proofreading.
- **Revision trail:** original reasoning, diagnosis, revision, and reflective caption kept together.
- **Reflective caption:** an evidence-based explanation of what changed, why, and what the change shows.
- **Portfolio claim:** a claim about growth supported by identifiable features in more than one artifact.
- **Review tools:** Week 12 induction / evidence proportion; Week 16 rhetoric-vs-proof (force may exceed or replace proof without making emotion “fake”).

Personalized answers are expected. Grade the required reasoning structure and the student's cited evidence; do not require one predetermined artifact, title, week, or revision path. Accept ordinary-language labels when logical roles are clear.

## Question 1: Part 1 Q1 – Identify and recover an artifact (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Names one completed course artifact by assignment ID, title, week, or other recoverable label; states its original claim or task; and quotes or accurately describes one concrete feature of the student's original reasoning.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Identification by ID (e.g. U1L12LL), short title, week number plus task type, or another label a teacher could recover. Original task may be restated as a question, thesis, conclusion, or assignment aim. The original feature may be a quoted line, a paraphrase of a premise/support move, a shifting term, an overclaim, a weak analogy, an untested source, or another specific reasoning detail.
- **Required meaning:** Recoverable identity **and** original claim/task **and** one concrete original-reasoning feature. “My logic lab” / “an essay” with no recoverable detail fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Identifiable artifact, original task/claim, and concrete original feature
- 1 pt: Artifact identified but task/claim or original feature is vague or incomplete
- 0 pts: No recoverable artifact, pure invention, or general discussion with no original feature

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require a formal assignment ID if title/week/task makes the piece recoverable.
- Do not require a long quotation; accurate paraphrase of one feature is enough when the prompt allows quote **or** accurate description.
- Do not require the student to criticize the artifact here—only identify and recover.
- Do not accept a polished later rewrite presented as if it were the original feature.

## Question 2: Part 1 Q2 – Justify the revision candidate (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Explains a visible before-and-after growth possibility (for example: clarify a shifting term, narrow an overclaim, repair an inference, test a source, replace irrelevant support, or deepen a literary analysis). Score alone is not treated as sufficient evidence.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Any specific logical growth contrast tied to this artifact: tighter claim, more stable definition, better evidence proportion, clearer assumption, fixed point-at-issue, improved source judgment, less circular restatement, rhetoric distinguished from proof, etc. Ordinary language is fine.
- **Required meaning:** A specific, logically meaningful growth contrast the revision could make visible. “It got a low/high grade,” “I like it,” or neatness alone fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Names a specific, logically meaningful before-and-after growth contrast
- 1 pt: Plausible candidate with only a vague growth claim
- 0 pts: Chooses only because of grade, neatness, preference, or length

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the student to use the word “before-and-after” if the contrast is clear.
- Do not require naming the eventual revised wording yet—only why the piece is a useful revision candidate.
- Accept brief answers when the contrast is concrete.

## Question 3: Part 2 Q1 – Recover the reasoning (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** States the artifact's conclusion, its evidence/premises/support, and one unstated assumption or inferential bridge connecting support to conclusion.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Conclusion may be called claim, thesis, main point, or judgment. Support may be called evidence, premises, reasons, or grounds. The bridge may be called hidden assumption, unstated premise, inference, warrant, or “what has to be true for this to follow.” Ordinary vocabulary is fine if the three logical roles are distinguishable.
- **Required meaning:** All three jobs—conclusion, support, and connecting assumption/inference. Plot summary or topic summary without logical recovery fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Conclusion, support, and assumption/inference are coherent and distinct
- 1 pt: Two elements clear, or all three present but thin/muddled
- 0 pts: Summary without logical recovery, or confuses support with the hidden bridge so no third element appears

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require formal labels “conclusion / support / hidden assumption” if the roles are clear.
- Do not reclassify a reason printed or clearly stated in the artifact as the hidden assumption; the third element must be a further bridge not already presented as the main support.
- Do not require a complete formal syllogism unless the artifact itself was one.

## Question 4: Part 2 Q2 – Diagnose by criterion (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Names one exact weakness or limit, a fitting Whately criterion, and the original feature the criterion tests.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Criteria may use course names or ordinary equivalents that preserve the same test, including: term stability / shifting word sense; proposition precision / overbroad claim; validity/form / bad inference shape; premise truth / doubtful fact; hidden assumption; circularity / restating the conclusion; point-at-issue / missing the point / irrelevant proof; analogy / disanalogy; induction / sample size / evidence proportion; source credibility / testimony; dispute classification; rhetoric-vs-proof / emotion as proof; motivated reasoning / self-deception. One precise weakness is enough.
- **Required meaning:** Specific feature **and** fitting criterion **and** accurate application to that feature. A tool name with no application, or vague self-criticism (“it was weak”), fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Precise feature, fitting criterion, and accurate diagnosis
- 1 pt: Real weakness but weak criterion fit or little textual specificity
- 0 pts: Vague self-criticism, tool name without application, or diagnosis that invents a flaw not present in the recovered reasoning

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require one official closed list of criterion names; accept clear ordinary-language equivalents that preserve the logical test.
- Do not require multiple weaknesses; the prompt asks for one.
- Do not require harsh self-judgment—precision beats severity.

## Question 5: Part 3 Q1 – Revise the claim (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** A revised claim/conclusion that directly answers the diagnosis and stays proportionate to what the original evidence can honestly support (narrow, qualify, clarify, or otherwise repair).
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Narrower scope; probability language; defined key term; dropped false necessity; claim limited to the available cases/sources; other repairs that respond to the stated diagnosis. Wording need not match any model sentence.
- **Required meaning:** Substantive response to the diagnosis **and** supportable by the artifact's available (or honestly limited) evidence. Pure copyedit, synonym swap, or unrelated new topic fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Substantive, responsive, supportable revised claim
- 1 pt: Directionally improved but still vague, overbroad, or only partly tied to the diagnosis
- 0 pts: Copyedit only, unrelated replacement, or claim that ignores the diagnosis

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require both narrowing and qualifying if one honest repair fixes the diagnosed problem.
- Do not require the student to keep every word of the original claim.
- Accept concise claims; elegance is not scored.

## Question 6: Part 3 Q2 – Revise support or inference (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Changes the reasoning itself so the revised support/inference helps earn the revised claim in Question 3.1: add relevant evidence, remove irrelevant material, stabilize a term, make an assumption explicit, qualify an induction, test a source, repair the inferential bridge, or state what evidence is still needed.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Any concrete change to evidence, inference, assumption, term, source test, or scope-connector that strengthens the claim-support link. Explaining a remaining evidence gap counts when it honestly limits what the claim can earn.
- **Required meaning:** A substantive reasoning change connected to earning the preceding revised claim. Cosmetic wording edits or unsupported new assertions fail.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Concrete revision strengthens the claim-support connection
- 1 pt: Useful change with incomplete connection to the revised claim
- 0 pts: Cosmetic edit, pure restatement, or unsupported assertion

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require adding brand-new external research if clarifying, qualifying, removing irrelevance, or stating a remaining need is the honest repair.
- Do not require multiple kinds of change; one well-aimed revision is enough.
- The prompt allows add, remove, clarify, qualify, **or** reconnect—do not require all of them.

## Question 7: Part 4 Q1 – Reflective caption (3 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** A reflective caption that covers the four prompt jobs: (1) what the original attempted, (2) what changed, (3) which logical habit guided the change, and (4) what the revision now shows about judgment.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Compact paragraph or labeled bullets. “What changed” may include the diagnosed weakness and the before-and-after contrast in one move. Logical habit may be a course tool name or a clear ordinary description of the habit (e.g. “I checked whether my sample could support a universal claim”). Honest limited growth is stronger than unsupported claims of total transformation.
- **Required meaning:** All four prompt jobs with enough concrete before-and-after evidence that a reader can see the trail. Praise, apology, or topic summary alone fails.

**Rubric:**
- 3 pts: Complete, evidence-based caption with all four major elements
- 2 pts: Sound caption missing one major element, or all present but one thin
- 1 pt: General reflection with little before-and-after evidence
- 0 pts: Praise, apology, or summary only

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require five separate labeled sections if the four prompt jobs are present.
- Do not require the model’s exact order.
- Do not withhold credit solely because the student frames diagnosis inside “what changed.”
- Do not require claims of dramatic transformation; accurate limited growth earns full credit.

## Question 8: Part 4 Q2 – Connect two artifacts (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Identifies a second portfolio artifact and one defensible recurring habit or growth pattern, with concrete evidence from **both** artifacts.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Patterns such as narrower claims, stable definitions, tested assumptions, proportionate evidence, improved source judgment, clearer point-at-issue, less circular support, better rhetoric-vs-proof separation, or another habit both pieces can support. Second artifact may be identified by ID, title, week, or recoverable label.
- **Required meaning:** Second artifact **and** one coherent pattern **and** evidence linked to each piece. Two titles with no growth connection fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Coherent pattern plus concrete evidence from both artifacts
- 1 pt: Pattern plausible but one artifact/evidence link is vague
- 0 pts: Two titles listed without a growth connection, or pattern with evidence from only one piece

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the second artifact to be from a specific week.
- Do not require equal paragraph length for each artifact.
- Do not require more than one pattern.

## Question 9: Part 5 Q1 – Review: transit induction (1 point)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** Improvement: the revision narrows a system-wide universal overclaim to a limited route-and-trip claim proportionate to three observations (induction / evidence-proportion tool). Remaining need: more representative evidence (more dates, times, routes, or system records) before a broader reliability conclusion.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Improvement language: overclaim fixed; sample no longer treated as whole system; claim sized to three trips/one route; less hasty generalization. Remaining need: larger sample; other routes; other times/days; system data; counterexamples checked; not enough cases for “entire system.” Tool may be named induction, sample size, evidence proportion, hasty generalization, overclaim, or described in ordinary language.
- **Required meaning:** Both jobs—(1) the logical improvement (proportionate narrowing) **and** (2) a real remaining evidence need. “The wording is better” alone fails.

**Rubric:**
- 1 pt: Names both proportionate narrowing (or equivalent induction improvement) and a real evidence need
- 0 pts: Says only that the wording is better, names only one of the two jobs, or treats three trips as enough for the whole system

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require the formal phrase “evidence proportion” if the sizing idea is clear.
- Do not require a long list of missing data types; one concrete remaining need is enough.
- Do not accept “the new sentence is shorter” without the logical improvement.

## Question 10: Part 5 Q2 – Review: rhetoric and proof (2 points)

**Expected Answer:**
- **Canonical answer:** A repaired judgment separates emotional force from evidence: the speech’s emotion does not itself prove the safety claims, but emotion also does not show that no evidence exists. The evaluator must inspect the stated reasons/data and decide whether rhetorical force matches, exceeds, or replaces them.
- **Acceptable alternatives:** Repair moves: emotion ≠ proof; pathos can move an audience without settling facts; check the actual reasons/data; force may exceed proof without making the speech “fake” or empty of all evidence; withhold judgment on safety claims until reasons are examined. Tool may be named rhetoric-vs-proof, logos/ethos/pathos, persuasion ≠ proof, or described in ordinary language.
- **Required meaning:** Rejects **both** extremes—(a) emotion as proof of the safety claims, and (b) emotion as proof that no evidence exists—and supplies a valid test (inspect reasons/data / match force to proof). Repeating either extreme fails.

**Rubric:**
- 2 pts: Rejects both emotion-as-proof and emotion-means-no-proof, with a valid evidence test
- 1 pt: Correct distinction but incomplete repair (e.g. only rejects one extreme, or names the problem without a test)
- 0 pts: Repeats either extreme, or treats emotion as automatically decisive either way

**Notes for grading:**
- Do not require all three appeal labels (logos/ethos/pathos) unless the student uses them accurately as the repair.
- Do not require deciding whether the principal’s safety claims are true; the task is to repair the judgment rule.
- Do not accept “emotion is fake” or “emotion is irrelevant to audiences” as the full repair—the course point is force vs proof, not cynicism about rhetoric.

**Overall Notes for Grading This Lab:**
- Whole points only. Total: 2+2+2+2+2+2+3+2+1+2 = 20 points.
- Reward verifiable self-assessment, not harshness toward earlier work.
- Do not require one predetermined artifact, one official criterion name, or model wording when the required logical jobs are present.
